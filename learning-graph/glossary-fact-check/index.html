    Glossary Fact-Check Report¶

    Checked on 2026-10-03. Of the 380 glossary entries, 111 contain numbers. Each number was checked in one of three ways: against a cited source, by arithmetic, or flagged as an illustrative value that no source supports.

    Corrections Made¶

    Glossary term Original claim Finding Source
    Snow Loads Minnesota ground snow 40 to 60 psf Minnesota uses 50 psf in most counties and 60 psf in 29 northern counties. Roof load is 0.7 × ground load. Minnesota DLI snow load map (Rules 1303.1700)
    Snow Loads 50 psf is "about 3 ft of fresh snow" Unsupported. Replaced with the 0.7 roof-load factor example. Same map
    Frost Depth "42 in. or more"; 60 in. "in some areas" Without an engineer's determination, minimum footing depth is 5 ft in Zone I (northern counties) and 3.5 ft (42 in.) in Zone II. Minnesota Rules 1303.1600 (UpCodes), Revisor version
    Allowable Building Area Wood-frame store limited to 12,500 sq ft Not in IBC Table 506.2. Group M, Type VB is 9,000 sq ft unsprinklered (Type VA is 14,000). IBC 2018 Chapter 5, Minnesota edition (UpCodes)
    Soil Bearing Capacity Firm clay about 2,000 psf The IBC presumptive value for clay, sandy clay, silty clay, clayey silt, silt and sandy silt is 1,500 psf. The spread-footing example was recalculated. IBC Table 1806.2 (UpCodes)
    Local Materials Benchmark of 500 miles 500 miles was the older LEED benchmark. LEED v4 uses 100 miles. GBRI: LEED v4 regional materials
    National Electrical Code Minnesota adopts it "with amendments" Minnesota Rules chapter 1315 adopts NFPA 70 by reference. The sources found did not show state amendments, so the claim was removed. Minnesota Rules ch. 1315
    Rot and Decay Needs moisture above about 20% Decay generally needs wood above about 25 to 30% (near fiber saturation). Below 20% is a common protective limit, not the decay threshold. USDA Wood Handbook, Chapter 14
    Live Loads 40 psf for residential floors 40 psf applies to most areas of one- and two-family dwellings. Sleeping rooms and habitable attics are 30 psf. IBC 2018 Chapter 16, Minnesota edition (UpCodes)
    Fiberglass Insulation R-19 batts fit between 2x6 ceiling joists A 2x6 is 5.5 in. deep, so R-19 (about 6.25 in.) would be compressed. Replaced with R-13 batts in 2x4 walls. PS 20 lumber sizes (NIST)
    Low-Voltage Systems "50 volts or less" NEC Article 720 says less than 50 volts. Note that Article 720 was removed from the 2023 NEC. NEC Article 720 (UpCodes)
    Mineral Wool Insulation R-3.7 to R-4.2 per inch Published range is about R-3.7 to R-4.3. Insulation R-value comparison
    Spray Foam Insulation Closed-cell R-6 to R-7 per inch DOE sources give about R-6 to R-6.5. Changed to "about R-6". DOE Building America spray foam guide, DOE insulation guide
    Recycled Content Rebar "more than 90%" recycled CRSI reports typically more than 97%. Changed to "about 97%". CRSI
    Fire Damage Steel loses much strength above 1,100 °F Steel retains about 50% of its strength at 1,100 °F, the critical temperature in ASTM E119. Example reworded. AISC Engineering FAQ 11.2

    Confirmed by Arithmetic or Definition¶

    These examples follow directly from the numbers in them: 1,200 W ÷ 120 V = 10 A; 15 A × 120 V = 1,800 W; 100 W × 10 h = 1 kWh; 12 ft ÷ 360 = 0.4 in.; 20 × 20 ft × 50 psf = 20,000 lb; 20,000 lb ÷ 16 in.² = 1,250 psi; 0.01 ÷ 100 = 0.0001 strain; 300 ÷ 600 = 0.50 water-cement ratio; R-20 → U = 0.05; 1/4 in. = 1 ft scale gives 10 in. for 40 ft; 12 ft 6 in. = 3.81 m; 6.5 ppm/°F × 1,200 in. × 100 °F ≈ 0.8 in.; 14 in. of cellulose at R-3.5 ≈ R-50. The dew point at 70 °F and 40% relative humidity is about 44.5 °F by the Magnus formula. Standard unit facts (60 Hz alternating current, 94 lb cement bag, 8 by 8 by 16 in. block, 29,000 ksi) are textbook values.

    Not Independently Sourced¶

    The following values are plausible illustrative examples, and no source was found to confirm them. Treat them as teaching examples, not code values.

    • Minneapolis design heating load of 35,000 Btu/h (the ASHRAE 99% design temperature sources found ranged from -9 to -16 °F, which makes a load of this size plausible but not specific).
    • Heat recovery ventilator preheating 0 °F air to near 60 °F.
    • South-window solar heat gain coefficient of 0.45; 7 kW photovoltaic array; 200 amp service for a 2,000 sq ft house.
    • Footing and pier sizes (16 by 8 in. strip footing, 30 in. by 25 ft drilled pier, 4 ft mat), header and truss spans, crawl space height, and slab thickness.
    • Service life of asphalt shingles (20 to 30 years) and masonry (over 100 years).
    • The 3 in. vent stack through the roof (the International Plumbing Code uses this size in cold climates, but the code text was not retrieved).

    Open Items¶

    • A 2024 code change proposal would raise Minnesota minimum footing depths to 48, 60, and 72 in. in three zones. Recheck the frost depth entry when the state adopts a new rule.
    • The Minnesota energy code link points to the 2015 edition on UpCodes. The 3 ACH50 requirement is unchanged in later IECC editions, but confirm against the edition currently adopted.
    • Minnesota Rules chapter 1315 now references a newer NEC edition than the 2023 one. The glossary entry avoids naming an edition.
