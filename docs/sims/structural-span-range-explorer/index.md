## Description

Students set a span and see which framing systems can reach it, which are in their typical range, and which are possible but unusual.

## How to Use

1. Move the slider, or drag the red marker in the chart, to a span you care about. The headline counts how many systems reach it.
2. Look at the dark bars first. A system in its typical range is an ordinary choice; one in the light part of its bar is possible but unusual and usually costs more.
3. Click a system's name to read what it is, why you would choose it, and what to watch out for.
4. Untick Wood, Steel, or Concrete to compare one material at a time, and change Sort to rank the systems by how far they reach.
5. Drag the red marker, or use the slider under the chart, to set your own span. Rows that reach it stay dark; rows that do not fade out. Click a row's name for what it is, why to choose it, and what to watch out for.

## Lesson Plan

**Learning objective:** Select the framing systems that can span a given distance, and explain why a longer span usually means a deeper, costlier, or more specialized system.

**Suggested activities**

- Predict and test (10 min): before using the chart, list which systems you think can carry the 40 ft Riverbend girder, then check.
- Compare (10 min): find the span at which wood framing stops being typical and steel takes over. Explain what changes for the building at that point.
- Apply (10 min): choose a floor system for a 28 ft clear span with ducts running through it and justify the choice.

**Assessment**

- Name three systems that can span 40 ft and say which are typical at that span.
- Explain in two sentences why a sawn lumber joist cannot be the answer for a 30 ft span.

## What Ages in This Sim

Values are illustrative and were last reviewed as of **2026-10**.

**Timeless (physics and principles):**

- Deflection grows with the fourth power of the span, so doubling a span needs far more depth or stiffness.
- Longer spans trade simple, cheap members for deeper, engineered, or prefabricated ones.

**Check before relying on it:**

| Item | Basis | What to check |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Every span range on the chart | Illustrative round numbers from general design references; the chapters do not give them | Use the manufacturer's span tables or an engineer's calculation for a real project |
| Which systems exist as stock products | Common products at the time of writing | Confirm availability and depth limits with local suppliers |
