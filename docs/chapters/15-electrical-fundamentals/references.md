# References: Electrical Fundamentals and Building Service

1. [Electric power distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution) - Wikipedia - Traces electricity from transmission voltages through primary and secondary distribution to 120/240 V service. Supports the chapter's utility service and transformer concepts and explains why AC, with transformers, replaced early DC systems.

2. [Circuit breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_breaker) - Wikipedia - Explains how breakers detect overcurrent through thermal and magnetic action and interrupt the arc, unlike one-time fuses. Includes low-voltage miniature and molded-case types, matching the chapter's circuit breakers and panelboards discussion.

3. [Three-phase electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-phase_electric_power) - Wikipedia - Describes three voltages offset by 120 degrees, line-to-line versus line-to-neutral voltage, and wye and delta connections. Supports the chapter's treatment of commercial systems such as 208Y/120 V and 480Y/277 V.

4. Electrical Wiring: Residential (19th Edition) - Ray C. Mullin and Phil Simmons - Cengage Learning - Walks students through a complete house plan, sizing service, panelboard, and branch circuits with National Electrical Code references. Its plan-based worked examples make the path from service entrance to outlet concrete.

5. Mechanical and Electrical Equipment for Buildings (12th Edition) - Benjamin Stein, John S. Reynolds, Walter T. Grondzik, and Alison G. Kwok - Wiley - Its electrical chapters explain service, distribution, and voltage selection for architects and builders. It is most praised for plain-language explanations and for showing the space that electrical rooms require.

6. [Circuit components and types of circuit](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/electromagnetism/x4352f0cb3cc997f5:how-many-toy-batteries-would-it-take-to-start-a-car/x4352f0cb3cc997f5:they-are-all-around-and-inside-us-electric-circuits/a/circuit-components) - Khan Academy - Introduces source, conductor, and load in a closed loop, plus resistors and series versus parallel layouts. Reinforces the chapter's definition of an electrical circuit with clear diagrams.

7. [Ohm's Law (Electric Circuits I, Chapter 2)](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Electronics_Technology/Electric_Circuits_I_-_Direct_Current_(Kuphaldt)/02:_Ohm%27s_Law) - Workforce LibreTexts, Tony Kuphaldt - Relates voltage, current, and resistance through a water-pipe analogy, then introduces power in watts. Directly supports the chapter's physical ideas, and the analogy suits students new to electricity.

8. [Three-phase Power Systems](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Electronics_Technology/Electric_Circuits_II_-_Alternating_Current_(Kuphaldt)/07%3A_Polyphase_AC_Circuits/7.02%3A_Three-phase_Power_Systems) - Workforce LibreTexts, Tony Kuphaldt - Shows how a 120/208 V three-phase source is built from windings 120 degrees apart. Explains why it needs smaller conductors than single-phase power, supporting the chapter's higher-voltage, lower-current argument.

9. [Electricity explained: Delivery to consumers](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/delivery-to-consumers.php) - U.S. Energy Information Administration - Describes how high-voltage transmission lines, substation transformers, and lower-voltage distribution lines carry power from plant to customer. Gives the utility-side context for the chapter's utility service and service entrance concepts.

10. [Electrical contractors](https://www.dli.mn.gov/business/electrical-contractors) - Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry - Describes how Minnesota adopts the National Electrical Code through its Board of Electricity, licenses electricians and contractors, and inspects work. Shows students why only licensed professionals install the equipment this chapter describes.
