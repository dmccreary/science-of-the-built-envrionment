# References: Enclosure Control Layers and Insulation

1. [Building envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_envelope) - Wikipedia - Defines the envelope as the separator between conditioned and unconditioned space and discusses water, vapor, air, and thermal control, giving students a concise overview of the control layers this chapter develops.

2. [Building insulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_insulation) - Wikipedia - Covers how insulation reduces heat flow, with sections on cold climates, thermal bridging, and material classes, supporting the chapter's comparison of fiberglass, mineral wool, cellulose, spray foam, and rigid foam.

3. [Vapor barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_barrier) - Wikipedia - Explains vapor retarders by permeability class and placement by climate, and clarifies the common confusion between vapor barriers and air barriers, which this chapter treats as separate control layers.

4. Builder's Guide to Cold Climates: Details for Design and Construction (1st Edition) - Joseph Lstiburek - Building Science Press - Lstiburek frames wall design around separate water, air, vapor, and thermal control layers, with cold-climate details showing where each layer sits, the logic this chapter adopts.

5. Building Science for Building Enclosures (1st Edition) - John Straube and Eric Burnett - Building Science Press - Straube and Burnett explain heat, air, and moisture flow with a unified physics framework and many worked wall examples, making vapor diffusion and condensation risk understandable to students.

6. [BSI-001: The Perfect Wall](https://buildingscience.com/documents/insights/bsi-001-the-perfect-wall) - Building Science Corporation - Lstiburek's short essay ranks rain, air, vapor, and thermal control layers and places them outside the structure, presenting institutional, commercial, and residential wall designs that anchor the chapter's wall assemblies.

7. [BSD-106: Understanding Vapor Barriers](https://buildingscience.com/documents/digests/bsd-106-understanding-vapor-barriers) - Building Science Corporation - Defines Class I to III vapor retarders by perm rating and gives climate-specific guidance, including very cold zones, with sixteen illustrated wall assemblies that show where vapor control belongs.

8. [Condensation Control for Walls in Cold Weather](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/condensation-control-walls-cold-weather) - Building America Solution Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Provides a table of exterior continuous insulation to cavity insulation ratios by climate zone through zone 8, directly supporting the chapter's continuous insulation discussion for cold climates.

9. [Types of Insulation](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/types-insulation-0) - Building Science Education Solution Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Introduces R-value and catalogs fiberglass, mineral wool, cellulose, foam, ICFs, and SIPs, with recommended products for foundations, walls, and roofs, summarizing the insulation materials covered here.

10. [BSI-142: Foundation Insulation Protection](https://buildingscience.com/documents/building-science-insights/bsi-142-foundation-insulation-protection) - Building Science Corporation - Lstiburek explains how to protect exterior foundation insulation from water and termites using flashing, cover boards, and inspection strips, relevant to frost-protected foundations and below-grade insulation.
