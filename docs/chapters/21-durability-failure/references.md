# References: Durability, Maintenance, and Building Failure

1. [Forensic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_engineering) - Wikipedia - Surveys how investigators determine causes of failures in materials and structures, from the 1847 Dee bridge collapse to modern microscopy and photogrammetry. Directly supports the chapter's building forensics and building failure concepts.

2. [Adaptive reuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_reuse) - Wikipedia - Explains converting existing buildings to new uses, with benefits, challenges, methodology, and global examples. Relevant to the chapter's adaptive reuse and renovation concepts, including embodied-energy and heritage arguments for keeping old structures.

3. [Frost weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost_weathering) - Wikipedia - Explains how freezing water fractures porous material through ice segregation and volumetric expansion, including the roughly nine percent expansion of ice. Gives the physical mechanism behind the chapter's freeze-thaw damage in Minnesota masonry and concrete.

4. How Buildings Learn: What Happens After They're Built (First Edition) - Stewart Brand - Viking Press - Brand popularized "shearing layers" (site, structure, skin, services, space plan, stuff), adapting Frank Duffy's idea. Each layer changes at its own pace, a clear lens for service life, maintenance, and adaptive reuse.

5. Why Buildings Fall Down: How Structures Fail (First Edition) - Matthys Levy and Mario Salvadori - W. W. Norton - Levy and Salvadori explain structural failures through readable case studies and everyday analogies, without heavy mathematics. Their stories of settlement, decay, and fire illustrate the chapter's building failure and forensics concepts.

6. [RR-0103: Water Management](https://buildingscience.com/documents/reports/rr-0103-water-management/view) - Building Science Corporation, Joseph Lstiburek - Presents the principle of shedding water by layering materials downward and outward, from site grading through roofs, walls, and openings. A concise guide to preventing water intrusion, rot, and mold.

7. [A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Explains that controlling moisture is the key to preventing mold, with cleanup guidance and moisture-control steps. Supports the chapter's mold, water intrusion, and preventive maintenance topics with practical homeowner-level detail.

8. [Preservation Brief 39: Holding the Line: Controlling Unwanted Moisture in Historic Buildings](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1739/upload/preservation-brief-39-controlling-moisture.pdf) - National Park Service - Diagnoses moisture sources in older buildings and describes signs such as efflorescence, eroding surfaces, and freeze-thaw spalling. Directly relevant to the chapter's efflorescence, freeze-thaw, and building assessment concepts.

9. [Best Practices for Reducing, Reusing, and Recycling Construction and Demolition Materials](https://www.epa.gov/smm/best-practices-reducing-reusing-and-recycling-construction-and-demolition-materials) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Covers design for disassembly and deconstruction as alternatives to demolition, including which building types suit salvage and how to handle hazards like asbestos and lead. Supports the chapter's deconstruction concept.

10. [Preservation Briefs](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1739/preservation-briefs.htm) - National Park Service Technical Preservation Services - Index of 50-plus technical guides on masonry, wood, roofing, and mechanical systems in older buildings, plus guidance on building codes for existing buildings. A practical resource for the chapter's renovation, retrofit, and adaptive reuse topics.
