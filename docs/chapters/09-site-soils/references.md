# References: Site Work, Soils, and Groundwater

1. [Bearing capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bearing_capacity) - Wikipedia - Defines ultimate and allowable bearing capacity and shows how soil strength, footing size, and groundwater affect foundation design. Directly supports the soil bearing capacity concept and the failure modes it illustrates.

2. [Frost heaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost_heaving) - Wikipedia - Explains how ice lenses grow in frost-susceptible soil, lifting pavements and foundations. Describes the conditions needed (freezing temperatures, fine soil, water supply), which explains why frost depth matters in Minnesota.

3. [Soil classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_classification) - Wikipedia - Introduces the Unified Soil Classification System and other schemes sorting gravel, sand, silt, and clay. Gives students the vocabulary for soil types and soil testing results.

4. Soil Mechanics in Engineering Practice (3rd Edition) - Karl Terzaghi, Ralph B. Peck, and Gholamreza Mesri - Wiley - Terzaghi's effective stress principle and bearing capacity analysis, with Peck's practical, experience-based commentary, explain how groundwater changes soil strength. Foundational for the chapter's soil and groundwater concepts.

5. An Introduction to Geotechnical Engineering (2nd Edition) - Robert D. Holtz, William D. Kovacs, and Thomas C. Sheahan - Pearson - Most praised for clarity, it builds soil classification, permeability, and bearing concepts with many worked examples and diagrams. Useful for students meeting soil testing for the first time.

6. [Web Soil Survey](https://websoilsurvey.nrcs.usda.gov/) - USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service - Free tool that maps soil units for most U.S. counties and generates reports on suitability, drainage, and properties. Students can define a site and perform a real site analysis.

7. [Minnesota Rule 1303.1600: Footing Depth for Frost Protection](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/1303.1600/) - Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes - The state code text setting minimum footing depths by frost zone and exceptions for slab-on-grade on suitable soils. Primary source for Minnesota frost depth requirements.

8. [Revised Builder's Guide to Frost Protected Shallow Foundations](https://basc.pnnl.gov/library/revised-builders-guide-frost-protected-shallow-foundations) - Building America Solution Center - HUD, PATH, and NAHB guide showing how perimeter insulation lets footings sit 12 to 16 inches deep in cold climates. Demonstrates a practical response to frost heave.

9. [Aquifers and Groundwater](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/aquifers-and-groundwater) - U.S. Geological Survey - Explains the zone of saturation, the water table, porosity, and permeability, with diagrams of aquifers and wells. Builds the physical understanding behind groundwater and site drainage concepts.

10. [Groundwater and Aquifers in Minnesota](https://extension.umn.edu/natural-resources/conservation/shoreland-watersheds-and-wetlands/groundwater-and-aquifers-in-minnesota) - University of Minnesota Extension - Describes glacial sand and gravel, sedimentary, and crystalline aquifers across Minnesota, plus unconfined versus confined conditions. Localizes groundwater concepts for regional site analysis.
