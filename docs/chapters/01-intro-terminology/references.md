# References: Introduction to the Built Environment and Construction Terminology

1. [Built environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment) - Wikipedia - Defines the human-made surroundings in which people live and work, from single buildings to infrastructure. It frames this chapter's nested scales and links design choices to public health, sustainability, and urban planning outcomes.

2. [Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) - Wikipedia - Surveys the construction industry's sectors, participants, and processes, including residential, commercial, industrial, and infrastructure work. Useful for the chapter's building-type categories and its overview of the people and trades involved.

3. [Architectural drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_drawing) - Wikipedia - Explains how plans, elevations, sections, and details communicate a design to builders. It supports the chapter's drawing vocabulary and includes examples showing how scale and conventions let one sheet describe a whole building.

4. Building Construction Illustrated (6th Edition) - Francis D. K. Ching - Wiley - Ching's hand-drawn axonometric and exploded views reveal how a building's components fit together layer by layer. Those drawings give students a visual model for the terminology of systems, assemblies, and materials.

5. Fundamentals of Building Construction: Materials and Methods (7th Edition) - Edward Allen and Joseph Iano - Wiley - Allen is most praised for organizing construction by material and method, with his own clear explanatory illustrations. This approach models how this chapter defines building materials, systems, and the shared language of the trades.

6. [BSI-152: What is Building Science?](https://www.buildingscience.com/documents/building-science-insights/bsi-152-what-building-science) - Building Science Corporation - A short essay separating building science from rules of thumb, rooted in physics and measured evidence. It directly supports this chapter's definition of building science and why the discipline matters to builders.

7. [Construction Document Sheet Numbers and Order](https://www.archtoolbox.com/construction-document-sheet-numbers/) - Archtoolbox - Explains how drawing sets are numbered and ordered under the National CAD Standard, with discipline letters and sheet types. It shows how plans, elevations, sections, details, and schedules are organized in a real set.

8. [SI Units](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/si-units) - NIST - The National Institute of Standards and Technology's reference for the International System of Units, including base units and prefixes. It supports this chapter's units-of-measurement concept and the conversions between metric and US customary units.

9. [Indoor Air Quality (IAQ)](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - The EPA's hub for indoor air topics, opening with the fact that people spend about 90 percent of their time indoors. It grounds the chapter's argument that the built environment shapes health and comfort.

10. [Construction Codes and Licensing](https://www.dli.mn.gov/about-department/our-areas-service/construction-codes-and-licensing) - Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry - Describes the Minnesota agency that administers the state building, energy, plumbing, and electrical codes and licenses contractors. It introduces the regulatory context of the construction industry that Minnesota students will work within.
