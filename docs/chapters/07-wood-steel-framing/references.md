# References: Wood and Steel Framing

1. [Framing (construction)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framing_(construction)) - Wikipedia - Explains balloon, platform, and post-and-beam framing, with terminology for studs, plates, joists, headers, and sheathing. Its comparison of framing methods gives students the vocabulary for wall, floor, and roof assemblies.

2. [Cross-laminated timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-laminated_timber) - Wikipedia - Describes how layers of lumber glued at right angles form large structural panels. Covers manufacturing, fire performance, and tall mass-timber buildings, which connects directly to engineered wood and glulam concepts in this chapter.

3. [Structural steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_steel) - Wikipedia - Surveys common steel shapes such as I-beams, channels, angles, and hollow sections, plus grades and standards. The labeled cross-section diagrams help students recognize the shapes used in beams, columns, and connections.

4. Building Construction Illustrated (6th Edition) - Francis D.K. Ching - Wiley - Ching's exploded axonometric drawings show each layer of wood platform framing, steel frames, and connections in assembly order, letting students see how members stack, bear, and tie together without reading dense text.

5. The New Science of Strong Materials, or Why You Don't Fall Through the Floor (2nd Edition) - J.E. Gordon - Penguin - Gordon explains wood as a cellular fiber composite and contrasts its tension and compression behavior with steel, using plain analogies that clarify why lumber, plywood, and steel members behave as they do.

6. [Wood Handbook: Wood as an Engineering Material](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62200) - USDA Forest Products Laboratory - The authoritative federal reference on lumber properties, wood moisture content, shrinkage, engineered wood products, and connections. Its tables of strength and moisture-related dimensional change underpin the chapter's lumber and moisture discussions.

7. [WoodWorks Resources](https://www.woodworks.org/resources/) - WoodWorks - Wood Products Council - A hub of solution papers, design examples, and case studies for light-frame and mass timber buildings. Useful for seeing glulam, CLT, and platform framing applied in real multi-story projects.

8. [OSB (Oriented Strand Board)](https://www.apawood.org/engineered-wood-products/plywood-osb/osb-oriented-strand-board/) - APA - The Engineered Wood Association - Covers OSB uses in subflooring, wall, and roof sheathing, with span ratings, exposure classes, and load-span tables. Supports the plywood and OSB concept and floor and roof framing choices.

9. [Advanced Framing: Minimum Wall Studs](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/advanced-framing-minimum-wall-studs) - Building America Solution Center - Describes 2x6 studs at 24 inches on center, a two-foot design grid, and reduced framing, with energy and cost data. Relevant to cold-climate wall studs and thermal bridging.

10. [Steel Framing Industry Association](https://www.steelframing.org/) - SFIA - Trade association for cold-formed steel, offering a technical guide, construction details, and a fire and acoustic directory. Supports the light-gauge steel framing concept with standard stud, track, and connector details.
