# References: Cladding, Windows, Doors, and Air Sealing

1. [Rainscreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainscreen) - Wikipedia - Traces rainscreen walls from 1960s Norwegian research and explains the drainage cavity and pressure equalization principle, supporting the chapter's treatment of cladding as a rain-shedding layer separated from the wall.

2. [Low emissivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-emissivity) - Wikipedia - Explains emissivity and how thin coatings on glass cut radiant heat loss, with a table comparing material emissivities, which supports the chapter's low-E coatings and glazing sections.

3. [Blower door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blower_door) - Wikipedia - Describes blower door history, the power-law airflow model, and metrics such as CFM50 and ACH50, giving students the underlying physics for the chapter's air leakage test.

4. Fundamentals of Building Construction: Materials and Methods (7th Edition) - Edward Allen and Joseph Iano - Wiley - Allen's clear, hand-drawn construction detail drawings of wall sections, flashing, and window installation show how layers are sequenced, making cladding and opening details easy to follow.

5. Residential Windows: A Guide to New Technologies and Energy Performance (3rd Edition) - John Carmody, Stephen Selkowitz, Dariush Arasteh, and Lisa Heschong - W. W. Norton - Explains glazing performance through U-factor, solar heat gain, and low-E coatings, drawing on Berkeley Lab research, so students can compare windows quantitatively.

6. [Drainage Plane Behind Exterior Wall Cladding](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/drainage-plane-behind-exterior-wall-cladding) - Building America Solution Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Details house wrap, taped foam, and membranes behind siding, with air gaps for wood siding, a one-inch cavity for masonry veneer, and stucco cautions, supporting the cladding sections.

7. [Windows and Doors Are Fully Flashed](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/windows-and-doors-are-fully-flashed) - Building America Solution Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Shows sill pan, jamb, and head flashing lapped over the drainage plane, with backer rod and caulk air sealing from inside, directly illustrating the chapter's flashing and sealants content.

8. [Purchasing Energy-Efficient Residential Windows, Doors, and Skylights](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/purchasing-energy-efficient-residential-windows-doors-and-skylights) - U.S. Department of Energy, Federal Energy Management Program - Defines U-factor, solar heat gain coefficient, visible transmittance, and air leakage ratings, with cold-climate selection advice, which supports the chapter's window, door, and glazing performance concepts.

9. [Tight Air-Sealed Homes](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/tight-air-sealed-homes) - Building Science Education Solution Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Lists key air sealing locations such as window perimeters, rim joists, top plates, and penetrations, with blower door verification, giving a practical checklist for the chapter's air sealing section.

10. [Enclosure Leakage Test](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/enclosure-leakage-test) - Building America Solution Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Describes blower door procedures following RESNET Chapter 8, with single-point and multi-point CFM50 and ACH50 results, smoke and infrared leak finding, and code compliance uses.
