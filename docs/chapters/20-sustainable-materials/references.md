# References: Sustainable Building Materials

1. [Life-cycle assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_assessment) - Wikipedia - Explains the four ISO phases of LCA: goal and scope, inventory, impact assessment, and interpretation. Distinguishes cradle-to-grave from cradle-to-gate boundaries, which underpin the chapter's life-cycle assessment and product declaration concepts.

2. [Embodied energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embodied_energy) - Wikipedia - Defines embodied energy as the total energy needed to produce a good or service. Includes tables of embodied energy values for concrete, steel, timber, and insulation, supporting the chapter's material-comparison arithmetic.

3. [Cross-laminated timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-laminated_timber) - Wikipedia - Describes CLT panels of glued, crosswise-layered lumber, their manufacturing steps, mechanical properties, code status, and tall-building examples. Directly relevant to the chapter's mass timber, renewable materials, and carbon sequestration discussions.

4. Materials and the Environment: Eco-informed Material Choice (2nd Edition) - Michael F. Ashby - Butterworth-Heinemann - Ashby, originator of material-property selection charts, extends them to embodied energy and CO2 per kilogram. His eco-audit method gives students a clear, quantitative way to compare materials like those in this chapter.

5. Cradle to Cradle: Remaking the Way We Make Things (First Edition) - William McDonough and Michael Braungart - North Point Press - The authors popularized the idea that waste equals food, separating biological and technical nutrient cycles. This framing underlies the chapter's reuse, recycled content, material health, and deconstruction concepts.

6. [Embodied Carbon in Construction Calculator (EC3)](https://www.buildingtransparency.org/glossary/embodied-carbon-in-construction-calculator-ec3/) - Building Transparency - Introduces the free EC3 tool, which benchmarks upfront embodied carbon of construction materials using third-party verified Environmental Product Declarations. Helps students see how EPDs drive real purchasing comparisons across concrete, steel, and wood.

7. [BEES: Building for Environmental and Economic Sustainability](https://www.nist.gov/services-resources/software/bees) - National Institute of Standards and Technology - Describes NIST's software that scores about 230 building products using ISO life-cycle assessment and life-cycle cost. Shows how environmental and economic performance combine into one decision metric for material selection.

8. [Sustainable Management of Construction and Demolition Materials](https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-management-construction-and-demolition-materials) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Outlines source reduction, deconstruction and salvage, recycling, and recovered-material purchasing. Cites 600 million tons of U.S. debris generated in 2018, supporting the chapter's construction waste and reuse topics.

9. [Building Material Reuse and Recycling](https://www.pca.state.mn.us/business-with-us/building-material-reuse-and-recycling) - Minnesota Pollution Control Agency - Explains Minnesota's priorities for reducing, reusing, and recycling construction and demolition materials and why extending building life cuts emissions. Connects chapter concepts of salvage and construction waste to local Minnesota practice.

10. [Volatile Organic Compounds' Impact on Indoor Air Quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Lists VOC sources such as paints, varnishes, and building materials, along with health effects and exposure-reduction steps. Supports the chapter's material health and low-VOC product selection discussion.
