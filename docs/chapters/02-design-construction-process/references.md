# References: The Design and Construction Process

1. [Design-bid-build](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design%E2%80%93bid%E2%80%93build) - Wikipedia - Describes the traditional delivery method in which the owner contracts separately with a designer and a contractor, then bids the work. It covers sequence, advantages, and drawbacks, matching this chapter's central delivery method.

2. [Integrated project delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_project_delivery) - Wikipedia - Explains the collaborative delivery approach in which owner, designer, and builder share risk and reward under a multi-party agreement. It clarifies how IPD differs from design-bid-build and design-build in contracts and early collaboration.

3. [Critical path method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_path_method) - Wikipedia - Explains the scheduling algorithm that finds the longest chain of dependent activities, which sets the minimum project duration. It includes forward and backward pass examples that support this chapter's treatment of critical path and sequencing.

4. The Architect's Handbook of Professional Practice (15th Edition) - American Institute of Architects - Wiley - The AIA's handbook is credited with codifying the standard phases of schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding, and administration. It explains each phase's deliverables and the architect's role during construction.

5. Construction Project Management: A Practical Guide to Field Construction Management (6th Edition) - S. Keoki Sears, Glenn A. Sears, Richard H. Clough, Jerald L. Rounds, and Robert O. Segner - Wiley - Most praised for its field-level clarity, it works through critical path scheduling networks, submittals, requests for information, and change orders with practical examples that mirror the paperwork students will meet on real jobs.

6. [Project Delivery Systems: CM at Risk, Design-Build, Design-Bid-Build](https://www.construction-institute.org/project-delivery-systems-cm-at-risk-design-build-design-bid-build) - Construction Industry Institute - Research report RR133-11 comparing how each delivery system defines roles, responsibilities, and activity sequence. It gives the evidence base for this chapter's delivery methods and is paired with a selection workbook.

7. [An Owner's Guide to Project Delivery Methods](https://www.cmaanet.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/owners-guide-to-project-delivery-methods.pdf) - Construction Management Association of America - A plain-language PDF guide written for owners comparing design-bid-build, construction manager at risk, and design-build. It clarifies contract relationships and risk, helping students see the owner's decision-making role.

8. [Minnesota Statutes 16C.32: Design-Build Contracts](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/16C.32) - Office of the Revisor of Statutes, State of Minnesota - The state law defining design-build and its qualification-based and price-based selection processes. It shows how delivery methods are regulated in Minnesota public work, which makes the chapter's terms legally concrete.

9. [A Guide to Building Commissioning](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/pnnl-21003.pdf) - Pacific Northwest National Laboratory - A U.S. Department of Energy guide explaining how commissioning verifies that building systems perform to the owner's requirements. It describes activities in each project phase, linking directly to this chapter's commissioning and closeout concepts.

10. [Construction Manager / General Contractor](https://www.dot.state.mn.us/const/innovative-contracting/construction-manager-general-contractor.html) - Minnesota Department of Transportation - Explains how MnDOT uses a construction manager who advises during design and then builds the project. It is a Minnesota example of the construction manager at risk model discussed in this chapter.
