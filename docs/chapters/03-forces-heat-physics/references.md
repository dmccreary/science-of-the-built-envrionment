# References: Forces, Heat, and the Physics of Buildings

1. [Heat transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_transfer) - Wikipedia - Covers conduction, convection, and radiation with governing equations and everyday examples. It is a thorough companion to this chapter's second half, linking the three heat flow modes to insulation and building enclosure behavior.

2. [R-value (insulation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-value_(insulation)) - Wikipedia - Defines thermal resistance and its relation to U-value, with tables of common materials and a discussion of series layers. It supports the chapter's R-value calculations and the effect of thermal bridging on real assemblies.

3. [Stress (mechanics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_(mechanics)) - Wikipedia - Explains stress as internal force per unit area, including normal and shear components and units. It deepens this chapter's introduction to stress and strain, with diagrams that clarify how members carry load.

4. Structures: Or Why Things Don't Fall Down (2003 Da Capo reissue) - J. E. Gordon - Da Capo Press - Gordon explains stress and strain through vivid everyday analogies, such as strain energy stored in stretched materials and why ropes, beams, and arches behave as they do. His prose makes the physics intuitive.

5. Building Science for Building Enclosures (1st Edition) - John Straube and Eric Burnett - Building Science Press - Straube and Burnett organize enclosure design around control layers for heat, air, water, and vapor flow. Their treatment of thermal bridging and cold-climate assemblies grounds this chapter's heat flow concepts.

6. [BSD-011: Thermal Control in Buildings](https://buildingscience.com/documents/digests/bsd-011-thermal-control-in-buildings) - Building Science Corporation - A building science digest covering how insulation controls heat flow, and how thermal bridges can cut an assembly's effective R-value. It adds cold-climate practicality to this chapter's thermal resistance and bridging concepts.

7. [Heat Transfer](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/thermo/heatra.html) - HyperPhysics, Georgia State University - Rod Nave's concept map defines conduction, convection, and radiation with linked equations and calculators. It lets students move between the physical principles and worked numeric examples behind this chapter's heat transfer section.

8. [1.050 Solid Mechanics (Fall 2004)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-050-solid-mechanics-fall-2004/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - Free lecture notes, problem sets, and solutions on static equilibrium, force resultants, stress, and strain in structural members. It extends this chapter's forces and equilibrium material to the next level of engineering analysis.

9. [Insulation Fact Sheet (DOE/CE-0180)](https://web.ornl.gov/sci/buildings/docs/factSheets/Insulation-FactSheet-2008.pdf) - Oak Ridge National Laboratory and U.S. Department of Energy - Explains R-value, how insulation resists heat flow, and recommended levels by climate zone. Its charts help students connect the chapter's thermal resistance concepts to real insulation choices for cold regions.

10. [Recommended Home Insulation R-Values](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate/identify-problems-you-want-fix/diy-checks-inspections/insulation-r-values) - ENERGY STAR - Lists recommended insulation R-values by climate zone and building location, including attics, walls, and floors. It lets students compare this chapter's worked R-value examples against practical targets for Minnesota's cold climate.
