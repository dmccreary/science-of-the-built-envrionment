# References: Roof Assemblies

1. [Roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof) - Wikipedia - Surveys roof forms, supporting structure, weatherproof layers, insulation, and drainage. Useful for the chapter's opening vocabulary, especially the contrast between flat and pitched roofs and the five core functions every roof performs.

2. [Ice dam (roof)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_dam_(roof)) - Wikipedia - Explains how heat escaping into the roof melts snow that refreezes at the cold eave. Covers the mechanism, insulation and ventilation mitigation, removal methods, and why membranes are required at eaves in cold climates.

3. [Green roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_roof) - Wikipedia - Describes extensive and intensive vegetated roofs, their stormwater and thermal benefits, costs, and disadvantages. Directly supports the green roof concept and the chapter's warning that planted roofs add significant structural weight.

4. Fundamentals of Building Construction: Materials and Methods (7th Edition) - Edward Allen and Joseph Iano - Wiley - Allen's hand-drawn, layer-by-layer sketches of roof sections separate steep-slope water-shedding roofs from low-slope membrane roofs. Those sections show students exactly where deck, insulation, membrane, flashing, and drains sit.

5. Building Science for Building Enclosures (1st Edition) - John Straube and Eric Burnett - Building Science Press - Applies a systematic hierarchy of rain-control strategies, including drainage and drying, to roofs and walls. Its heat, air, and vapor flow analysis explains why cold-climate roofs condense and form ice dams.

6. [BSD-135: Ice Dams](https://buildingscience.com/documents/digests/bsd-135-ice-dams) - Building Science Corporation - John Straube's digest explains ice dams as a heat-loss problem, not a gutter problem. It recommends an airtight ceiling plane, generous insulation, and then ventilation, matching the chapter's priority order.

7. [Construct Roofs and Attics for Ice Dam Prevention](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/construct-roofs-and-attics-ice-dam-prevention) - Building America Solution Center - A Department of Energy guide with construction details for air sealing, insulation depth, baffles, and ventilated over-roofs. Includes step-by-step checklists and cold-climate drawings that builders can follow on a Minnesota job site.

8. [About Attic Ventilation](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate/do-it-yourself-guide/about-attic-ventilation) - ENERGY STAR - Explains how balanced soffit intake and ridge exhaust keep an attic cold in winter and relieve heat in summer. Pairs with the chapter's attic ventilation concept and warns against insulation blocking airflow.

9. [Using Green Roofs to Reduce Heat Islands](https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/using-green-roofs-reduce-heat-islands) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Describes extensive and intensive green roofs, how evapotranspiration cools the roof surface, and the stormwater benefits. Its cost and comparison sections help students weigh green roofs against cool roofs and conventional membranes.

10. [Types of Roof Membranes for Flat or Low-Slope Roofs](https://www.archtoolbox.com/flat-roof-membranes/) - Archtoolbox - Compares built-up roofing, modified bitumen, EPDM, TPO, PVC, spray foam, and liquid-applied membranes. Also covers minimum slope and tapered insulation for positive drainage, supporting the chapter's low-slope roofs and roofing membranes concepts.
