# References: Energy Efficiency and High-Performance Buildings

1. [Passive house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_house) - Wikipedia - Explains the Passivhaus standard built on superinsulation, airtightness, high-performance windows, and heat-recovery ventilation. Useful for the chapter's Passive House concept, with certification criteria and cold-climate performance data.

2. [Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_in_Energy_and_Environmental_Design) - Wikipedia - Covers the history, rating systems, certification levels, and criticisms of LEED. Supports the chapter's coverage of green building rating systems and LEED certification, including how credits and performance studies compare.

3. [Zero-energy building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-energy_building) - Wikipedia - Defines net-zero energy buildings and compares energy-harvesting and energy-conservation strategies. Relevant to the chapter's top rung of the efficiency ladder, with country-by-country implementation examples and definitions of site versus source energy.

4. The Passive Solar Energy Book (Expanded Professional Edition) - Edward Mazria - Rodale Press - Mazria turned passive solar design into rules of thumb and sizing charts, including sun-path diagrams and the direct-gain, thermal-storage-wall, and sunspace categories, so designers could size south glazing and thermal mass without computers.

5. Heating, Cooling, Lighting: Sustainable Design Methods for Architects (4th Edition) - Norbert Lechner - Wiley - Lechner organizes energy-conscious design as a three-tier hierarchy of load avoidance, natural energy, then mechanical systems. His climate-by-climate design strategies and daylighting chapters mirror this chapter's ladder from efficiency to high performance.

6. [Energy Conservation (Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry)](https://www.dli.mn.gov/licenses-permits-and-plan-reviews/building-plan-review/energy-conservation) - Minnesota DLI - Official hub for Minnesota's residential Chapter 1322 and commercial Chapter 1323 energy codes. Lists effective dates, the MN COMcheck compliance tool, and ASHRAE 90.1-2019 forms for energy code compliance.

7. [Minnesota Building Energy Code Status](https://www.energycodes.gov/status/states/minnesota) - U.S. Department of Energy Building Energy Codes Program - Summarizes which energy code editions Minnesota has adopted, effective dates, state contacts, and compliance software. Useful for comparing Minnesota's amended codes with the national model codes behind the chapter's code-minimum baseline.

8. [High-Performance Home](https://basc.pnnl.gov/building-science-measures/high-performance-home) - Building America Solution Center, DOE and PNNL - Defines a high-performance home as above-code efficiency combined with comfort, indoor air quality, durability, and safety. Links to detailed guides on insulation, air sealing, HVAC, and roofing best practices.

9. [Daylighting](https://www.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting) - Whole Building Design Guide, National Institute of Building Sciences - Describes controlled admission of natural light to reduce electric lighting loads. Covers glazing, shading, photosensor controls, and design strategies that support the chapter's daylighting concept and its lighting-energy savings.

10. [Phius: Passive Building Standard](https://www.phius.org/) - Passive House Institute US - Presents the Phius CORE and ZERO standards, which tailor passive building targets to each North American climate. Includes design tools such as WUFI Passive and retrofit variants relevant to cold-climate Minnesota buildings.
