# References: Concrete and Masonry

1. [Concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete) - Wikipedia - Thorough article on cement, aggregates, the water-cement ratio, mix design, placement, and curing. Its sections on hydration and strength development tie together most of this chapter's materials concepts.

2. [Reinforced concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforced_concrete) - Wikipedia - Explains why steel bars are added to concrete, which is strong in compression but weak in tension. Covers rebar, prestressing, and corrosion, with diagrams of reinforced beams and slabs.

3. [Masonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonry) - Wikipedia - Overview of brick, stone, and concrete masonry units, mortar, and construction methods. Its coverage of bonds, load-bearing walls, and reinforced masonry gives students a broad vocabulary for the masonry half of the chapter.

4. Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures (16th Edition) - Steven H. Kosmatka and Michelle L. Wilson - Portland Cement Association - The standard industry reference, most praised for clarity. Its charts relating water-cement ratio to strength and durability, and its curing guidance, make mix design concepts concrete for students.

5. Why Buildings Stand Up: The Strength of Architecture - Mario Salvadori - W. W. Norton - Salvadori explains masonry arches, vaults, and reinforced concrete beams in non-mathematical language, showing how compression-only materials carry load and why steel reinforcement is needed where tension develops.

6. [Concrete Technology and Applications of Cement](https://www.cement.org/learn/concrete-technology) - Portland Cement Association - Describes ready mixed, precast, prestressed, and tilt-up concrete, along with concrete masonry units and insulating concrete forms. Helpful for comparing cast-in-place, precast, and prestressed systems.

7. [Mechanics and Design of Concrete Structures](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-054-mechanics-and-design-of-concrete-structures-spring-2004/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - Free undergraduate course by Prof. Oral Buyukozturk with lecture notes and problem sets on concrete behavior, reinforced members, and slab design. Offers a rigorous extension of this chapter's reinforced concrete topics.

8. [Rebar Properties](https://www.crsi.org/reinforcing-basics/reinforcing-steel/rebar-properties/) - Concrete Reinforcing Steel Institute - Explains why steel suits concrete: a uniform modulus, ductility, matching thermal expansion, and fire resilience, with yield strengths and ASTM specifications. Directly supports the reinforcing steel concept.

9. [Masonry Movement Joints](https://masonryadvisorycouncil.org/masonry-movement-joints/) - Masonry Advisory Council - Distinguishes expansion joints in brick from control joints in concrete masonry and works a spacing example. Matches the chapter's point that clay expands while concrete shrinks.

10. [Accommodating Expansion of Brickwork (Technical Note 18A)](https://www.gobrick.com/content/userfiles/files/tn18a-Accommodating-Expansion-of-Brickwork.pdf) - Brick Industry Association - Industry technical note on designing expansion joints to prevent cracking in brick walls. Offers placement guidance and details useful for the expansion joints and brick concepts.
