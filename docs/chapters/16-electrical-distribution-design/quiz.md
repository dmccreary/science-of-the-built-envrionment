# Quiz: Electrical Distribution, Lighting, and Design Team Coordination

Test your understanding of the concepts in this chapter with these review questions, one for each of the 23 concepts covered.

---

#### 1. Lighting expected to run continuously has a connected load of 8,000 VA. At what value should the equipment feeding it be sized?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 6,400 VA, which is 80 percent of the continuous load
2. 10,000 VA, which is 125 percent of the continuous load
3. 8,000 VA, which is the connected load
4. 1,000 VA, which is the demand factor
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A continuous load is any load expected to run for three hours or more, and the code requires equipment feeding it to be sized at 125 percent, the 80 percent rule seen from the other side. So 8,000 × 1.25 = 10,000 VA. The connected load is the sum of nameplate ratings as if everything ran at once, while the demand load is what the system is expected to carry at its peak.

#### 2. Which of the following is a responsibility of the electrical designer?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Designing the building's foundations, frame, and floors so they safely carry the structural loads
2. Designing the HVAC, plumbing, and fire protection systems and selecting their equipment and controls
3. Managing site construction, scheduling the trades, and deciding the means and methods of the work
4. Calculating loads, selecting and locating equipment, and documenting the design in drawings and Division 26
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The electrical designer plans the electrical systems, whether alone or under a licensed electrical engineer who takes professional responsibility. Responsibilities include power distribution, lighting, special systems such as fire alarm and data, documentation, and coordination and construction support, including submittal review and field observation. Structural design belongs to the structural engineer, mechanical systems to the mechanical engineer, and site management to the general contractor.

#### 3. Why must the electrical designer have the structural engineer approve penetrations through beams, joists, and slabs?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A hole in the wrong place can cut the capacity the engineer counted on, and the damage is hidden
2. Holes in structural members always cause fire hazards, so electrical wiring is banned from them
3. The structural engineer owns the conduit and is therefore responsible for pulling the wires through it
4. Penetrations affect only the cost of the project, and the structural engineer reviews the costs
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Electrical coordination with structural work covers four issues: equipment adds weight, wiring must pass through the structure, embedded conduit occupies space inside a concrete slab, and suspended equipment needs verified attachment points. At Riverbend, the glued-laminated beams over the 40 ft multipurpose room must never be drilled or notched without approval, so conduit is routed beside the beams or through located sleeves.

#### 4. What do the minimum circuit ampacity (MCA) and maximum overcurrent protection (MOCP) on an equipment nameplate tell the electrical designer?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. MCA is the largest breaker allowed, and MOCP is the smallest conductor rating allowed
2. MCA is the motor's mechanical capacity, and MOCP is its operating pressure
3. MCA is the smallest conductor rating allowed, and MOCP is the largest breaker allowed
4. MCA is the cooling load the unit adds, and MOCP is the heat it removes
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The mechanical engineer provides an equipment schedule listing each motor, fan, pump, boiler, and compressor with voltage, phase, and demand. The electrical designer uses the two nameplate numbers to select wire and breaker for each piece of equipment. The disciplines also share fire protection and controls, such as HVAC fans shutting down when the fire alarm sounds, and lighting and transformers add heat to the cooling load.

    **Concept Tested:** Mechanical Engineer

#### 5. Why is aluminum common in large feeders even though it has higher resistance for a given size than copper?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It has lower resistance than copper once it is above a threshold size, so it is preferred for feeders
2. It is lighter and cheaper, so the savings are significant on large conductors, and it is sized larger
3. It does not need any insulation or conduit, so large feeders made of it cost much less to install
4. It carries more current than copper of the same size because it is less dense and more flexible
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Building conductors are almost always copper or aluminum. A conductor must be large enough that its ampacity exceeds the load current after code adjustments, and the voltage drop must stay acceptable. Ampacity is reduced when many conductors share a conduit or when the ambient temperature is high. Insulation such as THHN/THWN-2 is rated for temperature and moisture.

#### 6. What defines low-voltage systems, and how are their cables handled?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. They operate at 480 V or above to supply large loads, and their cables are bundled with power conductors
2. They operate at 120 V for lighting only, and their cables are grounded at the panelboard with the power circuits
3. They operate at zero voltage, and their cables do not need to be listed or inspected by anyone
4. They operate at 50 V or below to carry signals, and their cables are generally kept apart from power conductors
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Low-voltage systems include data and communications, fire alarm, security and access control, audio and video, and building automation controls. They have thin conductors and low hazards, and different installation rules. Separation avoids electrical interference and keeps a fault in the power system from reaching the signal system. The designer often designs the pathways and power supplies, and specialty contractors install the devices.

#### 7. On an electrical plan, a receptacle is labeled "LP-2-9." How should an electrician interpret this?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It is receptacle 9 on lighting plan 2, which is one of nine on the sheet
2. It is the second lamp on the ninth floor, served by a main panel
3. It is circuit 9 on panel LP-2, which can be found on the panel schedule
4. It is a 2-pole, 9 A breaker located in the electrical room
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Electrical plans are scaled drawings on E-series sheets showing where equipment and devices are located and how they are connected. A line with arrowheads that extends toward a panel is a home run, and the label tells the electrician which breaker serves the device. Checking the circuit against the schedule catches errors on paper, before they become errors in the wall.

#### 8. In a congested ceiling, gravity pipes get priority, then large ducts, then pressure pipes, cable trays, and finally conduit. What explains this order?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Gravity pipes need a continuous slope and cannot bend around obstructions, large ducts are costly to resize, and conduit routes easily
2. Conduit carries the most dangerous loads, so it is placed last to keep it away from every other system in the ceiling
3. Ducts are the lightest elements, so they are installed first, and the heavier items are placed above them in the ceiling
4. Cable trays are the most expensive item, so they are always placed at the lowest level above the finished ceiling grid
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Interdisciplinary coordination compares drawings, resolves conflicts, and agrees on who occupies which space. Electrical work often yields in a conflict, but only when the designer has made sure a path still exists. Coordination happens in meetings, by exchanging drawings or models, by overlaying systems in a reflected ceiling plan, and by resolving requests for information.

#### 9. A 400 ft² room needs 50 footcandles at the work surface. With a coefficient of utilization of 0.70, a light loss factor of 0.80, and 4,000-lumen luminaires, how many are needed?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 5
2. 8
3. 9
4. 12
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The light needed at the work surface is 50 × 400 = 20,000 lumens. The fixtures must emit 20,000 / (0.70 × 0.80) ≈ 35,700 lumens, so 35,700 / 4,000 ≈ 8.9, rounded up to 9. Ignoring the losses gives 5 fixtures, and applying only one of the two factors gives 8. The result also determines the room's lighting power density, which energy codes limit.

#### 10. Why must a Minnesota designer check that the inverter's maximum voltage is not exceeded on the coldest morning?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Panels produce less voltage in cold weather, so the inverter might not start on the coldest winter morning
2. Panels produce more voltage in cold weather, so the array's open-circuit voltage peaks on the coldest morning
3. Cold weather raises the resistance of the conductors, so the inverter overheats and trips on the roof
4. Cold weather reduces the roof load, so the inverter's rating must be lowered to match the lower snow load
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A PV system has modules that produce DC, an inverter that converts it to AC, racking and wiring, and disconnects and meters. Code requires rapid shutdown so firefighters are not exposed to a live array. In cold climates snow also covers modules and adds to the roof load, and every roof penetration for mounting hardware must be flashed.

#### 11. What is the main benefit of clash detection in a BIM model?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It replaces the need for coordination meetings, so the disciplines no longer need to talk to each other
2. It automatically sizes the conductors and breakers for each load in the model without any designer input
3. It permits the electrician to install conduit through structural members without engineering approval
4. It finds elements that occupy the same space on the computer, where fixing them costs an hour, not a change order
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. BIM is a digital three-dimensional model in which each element carries data such as size, material, manufacturer, and connections. Each discipline builds its own part of the model, the parts are combined, and software identifies conflicts such as a cable tray passing through a duct. BIM also supports quantity takeoffs and facility management. It supplements coordination rather than replacing it.

#### 12. What is building automation?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A network of sensors, controllers, and software that monitors and controls building systems by schedule
2. A single thermostat that switches the heating system on and off according to the room temperature setting
3. A set of structural sensors that measure building deflection and warn the owner of overloaded floors
4. A group of low-voltage alarm devices that signal the fire department when smoke is detected in the building
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. A thermostat is the simplest example, and building automation extends the idea to hundreds of points. It can start fans at a set time, lower temperature setpoints on a night schedule, dim lights when daylight is available, and send an alert when a pump fails. Devices from different manufacturers commonly share data through open standards such as BACnet, and every sequence must be tested during commissioning.

#### 13. What does it mean for a fire alarm system to be supervised?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A licensed person must watch the panel at all times
2. The system automatically calls the fire department every day to test the line
3. The panel stops all alarms during testing so occupants are not disturbed
4. The panel detects a cut wire or a dead battery and reports a trouble signal
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. A fire alarm system has initiating devices such as smoke detectors, pull stations, and sprinkler flow switches; a fire alarm control panel; and notification appliances such as horns, speakers, and strobes. The panel also shuts down air handlers, closes smoke dampers, recalls elevators, and releases door locks on exit paths. Battery backup runs the system for hours. Design follows NFPA 72, and the fire marshal accepts the final system.

#### 14. Why must an elevator be coordinated with the fire alarm and emergency power systems?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. On an alarm the car is recalled to a designated floor for firefighters, and a standby generator often powers a car
2. On an alarm the car travels to the top floor and opens all of its doors to ventilate the shaft of smoke
3. On an alarm the car is disconnected permanently, and a generator is used only for the lobby and corridor lighting
4. On a power outage the car uses stored energy from the hoistway, so no coordination with other systems is required
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. An elevator is a car in a hoistway moved by an electric motor, either a traction elevator with cables over a sheave or a hydraulic elevator with a pump and piston. The electrical designer provides the power feeder, usually 480 V three-phase, a lockable disconnect, and lighting and receptacles in the machine room and pit. A 25 hp motor draws about 34 A at 460 V.

#### 15. An underground conduit carries wiring from outside into a warm electrical room in a cold climate. What detail is important?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Leave the end open to the electrical room so any water that gets in can drain out onto the floor
2. Bury it commonly 18 to 24 inches deep and seal it where it enters, so moist air and groundwater cannot enter
3. Bury it just below the surface so that it can be inspected for damage after each winter's freeze
4. Fill it entirely with conductors so no air or moisture can enter the conduit through its open end
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Conduit is a tube, usually metal or plastic, that protects conductors and routes them through the building. Types include EMT, rigid metal, PVC for underground and wet locations, and flexible metal for vibrating equipment. Conductors may fill no more than about 40 percent of the conduit so wires can be pulled and shed heat. Conduit routes must be coordinated with the foundation and enclosure systems.

#### 16. A hot wire touches a bonded metal enclosure through a 0.6 ohm fault path on a 120 V circuit. What fault current flows and what happens?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. About 4 A, which is far below the trip point, so the enclosure stays energized
2. About 72 A, which is high enough to heat the enclosure but not to trip the breaker
3. About 200 A, which trips the breaker almost instantly and de-energizes the enclosure
4. About 0.005 A, which is too low to be felt
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Fault current is I = V / R = 120 / 0.6 = 200 A, far above a 20 A breaker's trip point. Bonding connects all the metal parts of the system so a fault finds a low-resistance path back to the source. An enclosure connected only to a 25 ohm ground rod would carry just 4.8 A, so bonding, not the earth connection, is what trips the breaker.

#### 17. What is the correct sequence of the load calculation steps?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Sum the loads, round up to a standard size, list the loads, then convert them to VA and apply the factors
2. List the loads, convert them to VA, apply demand and continuous factors, sum and convert to amperes, then round up
3. Choose the service size, list the loads that fit it, apply the factors, then convert the result to VA
4. Apply the demand factors, list the loads, round up to a standard size, then convert the result to amperes
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The calculation converts a list of loads into the sizes of conductors, breakers, panelboards, and the service. After rounding up to the next standard size, the designer checks voltage drop and available fault current. The NEC provides a standard method and an optional method. A calculation done early guides the utility's transformer order, and one done at the end proves to the inspector that the equipment is adequate.

#### 18. Why is emergency lighting connected ahead of the lighting controls?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. So no switch, dimmer, or sensor can turn it off when normal power fails
2. So it can be dimmed by daylight sensors to save energy whenever the sun is shining
3. So it can be switched off by occupancy sensors in rooms that are unoccupied at night
4. So it follows the same time clock as the normal lighting in each part of the building
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Lighting controls include manual switches, dimmers, occupancy sensors, daylight sensors, and time clocks or networked controls. Energy codes commonly require automatic shutoff and daylight-responsive control in many commercial spaces, so controls are part of compliance as well as convenience. Emergency lighting must remain energized when normal power fails, so it is wired in a way that bypasses the controls.

#### 19. Where do codes commonly require GFCI and tamper-resistant receptacles?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. GFCI in corridors and stairwells, and tamper-resistant in mechanical rooms and storage closets
2. GFCI only in basements, and tamper-resistant only in garages and other detached accessory buildings
3. GFCI near water such as kitchens, restrooms, and outdoors; tamper-resistant in schools and child care
4. GFCI in electrical rooms, and tamper-resistant in elevators and above suspended ceilings
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A receptacle is a socket into which a plug connects to deliver power, and the general-purpose type is rated 15 or 20 A at 120 V. A GFCI trips when a few milliamperes leak to ground. Tamper-resistant types have shutters that block objects other than plugs. Outdoor receptacles must be weather-resistant with covers that protect them while a cord is plugged in, and mounting heights follow accessibility guidelines.

#### 20. Why do large buildings often need several telecommunications rooms linked by fiber-optic cable?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Fiber-optic cable carries power to the outlets, so it replaces all of the power wiring in a building
2. Copper cables cannot carry data in rooms above the first floor because of the added cable length
3. Telecommunications rooms are allowed only one per floor, so fiber is needed to link the floors
4. Twisted-pair copper links are limited to about 328 feet, so farther outlets need another room
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Structured cabling runs twisted-pair copper cable, such as Category 6, from outlets to a telecommunications room with network equipment in racks. Many devices, including wireless access points and cameras, receive power over the same network cable, called power over Ethernet. The designer must reserve rooms with adequate size, cooling, and power, and decide their location during schematic design.

#### 21. What does a one-line diagram show?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The routing of each individual conductor through every room, drawn to scale on the floor plans
2. The layout of luminaires, switches, and receptacles in each room, drawn on the reflected ceiling plan
3. The structural supports for each piece of electrical equipment, with anchor bolts and pad dimensions
4. The whole distribution system on one sheet, with a single line for a group of conductors and equipment symbols
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. A one-line diagram uses standard symbols for transformers, breakers, switchboards, and panelboards, and labels each device with its rating, such as 400 A main breaker or 225 A panel LP-1. It reads from the source to the loads. The utility uses it to understand the service, the inspector to check protection, and the electrician to build in the correct order. It also shows whether a feeder is protected by an appropriate breaker.

#### 22. Why are the electrical room's location and size, the service voltage, and space for risers best decided in the first two design phases?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. These decisions require stamped drawings, which are completed in the first two design phases only
2. Changes cost almost nothing early, and a late designer may find the electrical room already given to a closet
3. The utility cannot respond to any requests for service once the first two phases have ended
4. The building official approves these particular items only during programming, before any drawings exist
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Electrical design phases parallel the project phases: programming, schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding, construction, commissioning, and closeout. The cost of changing a design rises with each phase, as Chapter 2 explained. In schematic design the designer estimates service size, meets the utility, and locates the electrical room, and by design development chooses systems and lays out panels and lighting.

#### 23. A charger delivers 24 A and is treated as a continuous load. What minimum circuit rating is needed?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 30 A
2. 24 A
3. 20 A
4. 40 A
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Because charging runs for hours, the NEC treats an EV charger as a continuous load, so the circuit is sized at 125 percent of the charger's current: 24 × 1.25 = 30 A. A 32 A charger would need a 40 A circuit. Level 1 uses an ordinary 120 V outlet, Level 2 uses 208 or 240 V, and DC fast charging uses a dedicated three-phase supply. EV-capable, EV-ready, and EV-installed describe stages of preparation.

    **Concept Tested:** Electric Vehicle Charging

    **See:** [Electric Vehicle Charging](index.md#electric-vehicle-charging)
