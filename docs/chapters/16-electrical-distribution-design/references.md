# References: Electrical Distribution, Lighting, and Design Team Coordination

1. [Single-line diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-line_diagram) - Wikipedia - Explains how one line stands in for a multi-conductor power system, with standard symbols for buses, breakers, and transformers. It supports this chapter's one-line diagram section and shows why a whole distribution system fits on one sheet.

2. [Building information modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_information_modeling) - Wikipedia - Describes digital building models whose elements carry data, and how the trades combine their models to detect clashes. It supports the chapter's coordination discussion, with a history of BIM standards and lifecycle uses.

3. [Electrical wiring in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_wiring_in_North_America) - Wikipedia - Surveys United States and Canadian wiring practice, including conductor types, color codes, conduit and cable methods, and the National Electrical Code. It supports the chapter's treatment of conductors, conduit, grounding, and receptacles.

4. Electrical Wiring Commercial (17th Edition) - Ray C. Mullin and Phil Simmons - Cengage - Walks students through a complete set of commercial building drawings, sizing loads, conductors, panels, and services for that one building. Its plan-driven worked-example method parallels this chapter's Riverbend demand-load calculation.

5. Lighting Design Basics (3rd Edition) - Mark Karlen, Christina Spangler, and James R. Benya - Wiley - Teaches the lumen method as a plain-language, step-by-step procedure with illustrated worked examples for rooms of different types. It is the clearest companion to this chapter's classroom lumen-method example.

6. [Electrical One-Line Diagram](https://www.archtoolbox.com/electrical-one-line-diagram/) - Archtoolbox - Defines the one-line diagram for architects and building owners, showing standard graphic symbols for transformers, switches, and breakers and how power flow reads across the sheet. Useful preparation for interpreting the Riverbend diagram.

7. [Electric Lighting Controls](https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/electric-lighting-controls) - Whole Building Design Guide - Compares occupancy sensors, photosensors, dimmers, and clock switches, with a table matching control strategies to room types such as classrooms and restrooms. It also stresses commissioning and gives a payback formula.

8. [Charging Electric Vehicles at Home](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-home) - Alternative Fuels Data Center, U.S. Department of Energy - Explains Level 1 and Level 2 charging equipment, installation by certified electricians, permitting, and National Electrical Code Article 625. It also gives a per-mile cost example relevant to the chapter's EV charger sizing.

9. [Electrical Permits for Contractors](https://www.dli.mn.gov/business/electrical-contractors/electrical-permits-contractors) - Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry - Shows how Minnesota electrical permits are applied for online and how inspections are scheduled, with a lookup for state or local jurisdiction. It connects the designer's drawings to the permit and inspection process.

10. [Learn More About NFPA 70, National Electrical Code (NEC)](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/electrical/understanding-nfpa-70-national-electrical-code) - National Fire Protection Association - The publisher's introduction to the NEC, the installation standard behind this chapter's conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, and continuous-load rules. It points to the code's three-year revision cycle and related standards.
