# References: HVAC, Plumbing, and Fire Protection Systems

1. [Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating,_ventilation,_and_air_conditioning) - Wikipedia - Overview of heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and filtration, including boilers, heat pumps, refrigeration cycles, and ASHRAE standards. A good map of the chapter's HVAC vocabulary before students study individual equipment types.

2. [Heat pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump) - Wikipedia - Explains the vapor-compression cycle, coefficient of performance, and air-source, ground-source, and water-source types. Supports the heat pump concept and why modern variable-speed units can heat efficiently in cold climates such as Minnesota.

3. [Fire sprinkler system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_sprinkler_system) - Wikipedia - Describes sprinkler heads, thermal-bulb activation, and the wet pipe, dry pipe, deluge, and pre-action system types. Dry-pipe systems are especially relevant to unheated Minnesota spaces. Complements the chapter's sprinkler systems and active fire protection discussion.

4. Mechanical and Electrical Equipment for Buildings (12th Edition) - Benjamin Stein, John S. Reynolds, Walter T. Grondzik, and Alison G. Kwok - Wiley - Long the standard architecture-school text on building systems. It is most praised for clear diagrams and for treating HVAC, plumbing, and fire protection together as space-consuming systems.

5. Heating, Ventilating, and Air Conditioning: Analysis and Design (6th Edition) - Faye McQuiston, Jeffrey Parker, and Jeffrey Spitler - Wiley - Its worked examples take students step by step through psychrometric-chart reading and heating and cooling load calculations. This gives the chapter's load and ventilation concepts a quantitative foundation.

6. [Whole-House Ventilation Strategies for New Homes](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/whole-house-ventilation-strategies-new-homes) - Building America Solution Center - Compares exhaust-only, supply, and balanced ventilation systems with design guidance tied to ASHRAE 62.2. Directly supports the chapter's mechanical ventilation and heat recovery ventilator discussion for tight cold-climate buildings.

7. [Radon Resistant New Construction](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/radonresistant.html) - Minnesota Department of Health - Explains the passive sub-slab radon system required in new Minnesota homes: gravel layer, vent pipe through the roof, and a rough-in for a fan. A local illustration of the chapter's radon mitigation concept.

8. [Minnesota Plumbing Code frequently asked questions](https://www.dli.mn.gov/business/plumbing-contractors/minnesota-plumbing-code-frequently-asked-questions) - Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry - Answers common questions about drain sizing, fixtures, floor drains, and venting under the Minnesota Plumbing Code. Shows students how drain-waste-vent rules are interpreted and enforced in practice.

9. [How Fire Sprinklers Work](https://homefiresprinkler.org/how-fire-sprinklers-work/) - Home Fire Sprinkler Coalition - Describes the sprinkler as a plug held back by a heat-sensitive glass bulb or solder link, so only the head nearest the fire opens. Includes animated videos that clarify the chapter's sprinkler operation concept.

10. [Energy Saver: Heat Pump Systems](https://basc.pnnl.gov/library/energy-saver-heat-pump-systems) - U.S. Department of Energy, via Building America Solution Center - Consumer-level guidance on air-source, ground-source, and ductless heat pumps, including efficiency ratings and installation considerations. Helps students connect the heating and cooling systems concepts to equipment choices.
