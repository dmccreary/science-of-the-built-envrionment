# References: Fire Protection and Life Safety Requirements

1. [Fire-resistance rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-resistance_rating) - Wikipedia - Explains how assemblies are rated by the time they withstand a standard fire test, including time-temperature curves and testing standards. It supports the chapter's section on ratings applying to assemblies, not materials.

2. [Passive fire protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_fire_protection) - Wikipedia - Describes fire-rated walls, floors, doors, dampers, and firestopping that slow fire and smoke without activation. It supports the chapter's discussion of fire separations and protecting every penetration, with examples and a comparison of regulatory approaches.

3. [Emergency power system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_power_system) - Wikipedia - Surveys backup power for buildings, including generators, batteries, flywheels, and uninterruptible power supplies, with applications in hospitals and data centers. It supports the chapter's comparison of unit equipment, UPS, and generators.

4. Fundamentals of Building Construction: Materials and Methods (Latest Edition) - Edward Allen and Joseph Iano - Wiley - Its chapter on fire-safe construction lays out the five construction types and fire-resistance ideas with clear drawings. It is most praised for clarity, and it supports this chapter's construction-type table.

5. SFPE Handbook of Fire Protection Engineering (5th Edition) - Morgan J. Hurley, Editor - Springer - The Society of Fire Protection Engineers' reference develops the available-versus-required safe egress time framework, including detection, pre-movement, and travel time. That framework is the basis of this chapter's ASET and RSET worked example.

6. [IBC Occupancy Classifications: All 10 Groups Explained](https://academy2.youngarchitect.com/occupancy-classifications/) - Young Architect - Walks through the ten International Building Code occupancy groups with examples, mixed-use approaches (accessory, separated, non-separated), and common classification mistakes. It supports the chapter's Riverbend classification exercise.

7. [Using Char Methods to Demonstrate Fire Resistance of Exposed Wood Members](https://www.woodworks.org/resources/using-char-methods-to-demonstrate-fire-resistance-of-exposed-wood-members/) - WoodWorks - Explains the nominal 1.5 inch per hour char rate and the effective char depth used for glulam and other timber, up to two hours. It matches this chapter's glulam beam worked example.

8. [Technical Report No. 10: Calculating the Fire Resistance of Wood Members and Assemblies](https://awc.org/publications/tr-10-calculating-the-fire-resistance-of-wood-members-and-assemblies) - American Wood Council - The industry's technical report gives the mechanics-based method for residual section capacity of exposed wood members after charring. It is the detailed source behind this chapter's simplified section-modulus calculation.

9. [Rethinking Egress: A Vision for the Future (NIST Technical Note 1647)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/el/fire_research/Rethinking-Egress-Final-Report.pdf) - National Institute of Standards and Technology - Reviews how people actually evacuate buildings and how egress design, signage, and technology could improve. It deepens the chapter's treatment of means of egress, travel time, and occupant response.

10. [An Overview of NFPA 110](https://www.nfpa.org/news-blogs-and-articles/blogs/2023/01/23/an-overview-of-nfpa-110) - National Fire Protection Association - Introduces the standard for emergency and standby power systems, covering power sources, transfer equipment, and testing, and the Level 1 and Level 2 classifications. It supports the chapter's emergency power and generator sections.
