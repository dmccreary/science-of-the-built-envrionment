# References: Structural Loads and Load Paths

1. [Structural load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_load) - Wikipedia - Defines structural loads and classifies them as dead, live, and environmental loads, with notes on building codes and load factors. A concise overview of the load types and combinations this chapter develops.

2. [Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truss) - Wikipedia - Explains how triangulated members carry loads in tension and compression, with Warren, Pratt, and bowstring types and analysis methods. Illustrates roof and floor trusses as key parts of a gravity load system.

3. [Shear wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_wall) - Wikipedia - Describes shear walls as vertical elements that resist lateral wind and seismic forces, including construction types and design principles. Directly supports the lateral load system, diaphragm, and bracing concepts in this chapter.

4. Why Buildings Stand Up: The Strength of Architecture (Revised Edition) - Mario Salvadori - W. W. Norton - Salvadori explains how loads travel through beams, columns, arches, and walls with everyday analogies and almost no mathematics. He is most praised for making load flow and structural behavior intuitive for architecture students.

5. Building Structures Illustrated: Patterns, Systems, and Design (2nd Edition) - Francis D. K. Ching, Barry Onouye, and Douglas Zuberbuhler - Wiley - Ching's signature isometric drawings show how floor, wall, and frame systems carry gravity and lateral loads. Visual layouts make load paths and structural systems easy to follow.

6. [Continuous Load Path Provided with Connections from the Roof through the Wall to the Foundation](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/continuous-load-path-provided-connections-roof-through-wall-foundation) - Building America Solution Center (PNNL) - Practical guide to connecting roof sheathing, roof-to-wall, wall-to-wall, and wall-to-foundation joints so uplift, shear, and seismic loads reach the ground. Directly supports this chapter's structural connections and load path concepts.

7. [Chapter 1: Loads on Structures](https://structuraldesign.pressbooks.sunycreate.cloud/chapter/chapter-1/) - Anahita Khodadadi, Basic Concepts of Structural Design for Architecture Students (Open Textbook) - Free open-textbook chapter covering dead, live, wind, snow, and earthquake loads with density tables and load combination formulas. Written for architecture students, with practical examples like calculating a beam's self-weight.

8. [Minnesota Rules 1303.1700: Ground Snow Load](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/1303.1700/) - Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes - The state's official ground snow load requirements, assigned by county for structural design. Shows how snow loads are set for Minnesota buildings under the state building code amendments.

9. [Home Builder's Guide to Coastal Construction (FEMA P-499)](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/p-499_homebuilders-guide-coastal-construction.pdf) - Federal Emergency Management Agency - Fact sheet series with drawings and photographs on wind and flood loads, including dedicated fact sheets on load paths and structural connections. Although coastal in focus, its load path explanations apply to any high-wind setting.

10. [3.5 The Method of Joints](https://learnaboutstructures.com/Method-of-Joints) - Learn About Structures (Carleton University) - Step-by-step procedure with a worked six-joint truss example, free body diagrams, and tension versus compression identification. Gives students hands-on practice tracing how loads flow through truss members and joints.
