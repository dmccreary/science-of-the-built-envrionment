# References: Properties of Building Materials

1. [Strength of materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_of_materials) - Wikipedia - Surveys stress, strain, elastic modulus, yield, and ultimate strength, plus failure theories. Gives students the vocabulary behind tensile, compressive, and shear strength, stiffness, and toughness, with definitions and equations.

2. [Ductility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ductility) - Wikipedia - Explains ductility as the ability to deform plastically before fracture, quantified by percent elongation and reduction in area. Its ductile-to-brittle transition section, with the Liberty ship failures, shows why this property matters in cold climates.

3. [Fire-resistance rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-resistance_rating) - Wikipedia - Describes how assemblies are rated by the time they withstand a standard fire test, including the standard time-temperature curve and rating systems. Clarifies the chapter's distinction between combustibility and fire resistance.

4. The New Science of Strong Materials, or Why You Don't Fall Through the Floor (2nd Edition) - J. E. Gordon - Penguin Books - Gordon explains strength, stiffness, toughness, and brittle fracture in plain language, using the energy argument for crack growth. His distinction between strength and stiffness remains a classic way to teach these properties.

5. Materials Selection in Mechanical Design (4th Edition) - Michael F. Ashby - Butterworth-Heinemann - Ashby invented property charts that plot materials on log-log axes, such as modulus against density, with guide lines for performance indices. This gives a systematic, visual method for material selection that applies directly to building materials.

6. [Chapter 5: Mechanical Properties of Wood (Wood Handbook)](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62244) - USDA Forest Products Laboratory - Authoritative treatment of wood's tension, compression, shear, and bending properties, including how moisture, knots, and grain slope change strength. A rare real-world look at property variability within one material.

7. [3.11 Mechanics of Materials: Modules](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-11-mechanics-of-materials-fall-1999/pages/modules/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - David Roylance's free modules on tensile response, stress-strain curves, elasticity, and failure, with a materials-science emphasis. Useful for deepening modulus of elasticity, ductility, toughness, and creep beyond this chapter's introduction.

8. [Fire Standards and Flammability Standards](https://store.astm.org/products-services/standards-and-publications/standards/fire-standards-and-flammability-standards.html) - ASTM International - Catalog of ASTM fire tests organized by category, including combustibility, fire resistance, and surface burning. Shows how standard tests produce the numbers designers use, tying together material testing and ASTM standards.

9. [CIP 35: Testing Compressive Strength of Concrete](https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/35pr.pdf) - National Ready Mixed Concrete Association - Practical technical note on casting, curing, and breaking concrete cylinders under ASTM procedures to measure compressive strength. A concrete example of how a standard test turns a material property into a design number.

10. [Linear Thermal Expansion Coefficients of Materials](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/linear-expansion-coefficients-d_95.html) - The Engineering ToolBox - Reference table of expansion coefficients for metals, plastics, ceramics, and other materials, with the length-change relationship. Lets students compare steel, aluminum, and plastics when sizing expansion joints and cladding gaps.
