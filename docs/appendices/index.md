# Appendices: Topics Changing Fastest

!!! mascot-welcome "The Physics Stays Put While the Products Move"
    ![Beau waving welcome](../img/mascot/welcome.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    Chapters 1 through 21 teach the science that holds still: loads, heat, air, moisture, fire, and the ways buildings fail. These appendices cover the parts of the trade that will not hold still, and each one shows the unchanging physics underneath. Let's build it right!

The built environment is anything but timeless. Solar panels that were a luxury a generation ago now sit on ordinary roofs. Heat exchangers recover the warmth of the air a building exhales, batteries on a garage wall carry a house through an outage, and pipes buried in the ground borrow its steady temperature. Heat pumps now heat Minnesota buildings in January, and sensors report on a building's air, water, and energy around the clock. New materials, new codes, and new software arrive every year.

The laws of physics that govern how buildings work do not change. Heat still flows from warm to cold, loads still travel to the ground, and moisture still moves from wet to dry. That is why the main chapters teach the physics and why these appendices are built differently. Each one names what is changing, then shows the unchanging principle that lets you evaluate it, usually with a worked example you can repeat with next year's prices and products.

## How Fast Is Each Topic Moving?

Each appendix lists a `last_reviewed` date in its frontmatter and a "What to Watch" section naming the facts most likely to go stale. Rates of change differ.

- **Very high:** heat pumps and refrigerants (A), solar photovoltaics (C), battery storage (D), smart sensors and building automation (F), and digital design, prefabrication, and AI (I). Prices, products, and rules can change within a year.
- **High:** heat recovery ventilation (B), geothermal systems (E), mass timber and low-carbon materials (G), and energy codes and performance standards (H). Technology and rules change on a cycle of a few years.

## How to Read an Appendix

1. Start with the unchanging physics and the worked example; they will still be right in ten years.
2. Read "What Is Changing" as a snapshot, and check the date at the top and the references at the bottom before relying on a number.
3. Use "What to Watch" as a checklist when you read a news story or a product brochure on the topic.
4. Follow the "Connects To" links back to the chapters that build the prerequisite understanding.

## Keeping the Appendices Current

A printed textbook goes out of date the day it is printed. This book is built to be updated, and the plan is for intelligent agents to review these appendices on a regular schedule, check each claim against its source, revise what has changed, and update the `last_reviewed` date, with the author approving the result. Readers can help by reporting out-of-date information on the [Contact](../contact.md) page.

## Looking for Timeless Patterns

The goal of these appendices, like the rest of the book, is not to give you rules to memorize. Products and codes change, so the rules would be out of date quickly. The goal is to help you recognize the patterns underneath, the ones Christopher Alexander described in *A Timeless Way of Building*. You can read the [story behind that book](../stories/timeless-way-of-building/index.md) and the [About page](../about.md) for why this book takes that approach.
