# References: Heat Recovery Ventilation and Modern Heat Exchangers

1. [Heat recovery ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_recovery_ventilation) - Wikipedia - Explains how HRVs and ERVs recover heat and moisture from exhaust air. Compares core types and effectiveness ranges, and notes frost-control methods such as modulating wheel speed, preheating, and stop-jogging.

2. [Heat exchanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_exchanger) - Wikipedia - Surveys heat exchanger designs, including parallel, counterflow, and crossflow arrangements and plate types. Gives background for why counterflow cores reach higher effectiveness, and covers fouling that degrades performance.

3. [Thermal wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_wheel) - Wikipedia - Describes rotary energy-recovery wheels, the difference between sensible and latent (desiccant) transfer, and frost prevention in cold climates. Relevant to ERV cores and to defrost strategies like speed modulation.

4. Compact Heat Exchangers (3rd Edition) - W. M. Kays and A. L. London - McGraw-Hill - Kays and London popularized the effectiveness-NTU method, defining effectiveness as actual over maximum possible heat transfer and charting it for many flow arrangements. This is the basis of this appendix's sensible effectiveness formula.

5. Fundamentals of Heat Exchanger Design (1st Edition) - Ramesh K. Shah and Dusan P. Sekulic - John Wiley & Sons - Presents the effectiveness-NTU, P-NTU, and mean-temperature-difference methods in one unified framework. No single originator stands out here, so it is chosen as a standard, clearly organized reference for counterflow core design.

6. [Heat Recovery Ventilation in Colder Climates - Code Compliance Brief](https://basc.pnnl.gov/code-compliance/heat-recovery-ventilation-colder-climates-code-compliance-brief) - Building America Solution Center, DOE and PNNL - Summarizes 2015 IECC and IRC provisions for climate zones 6 to 8, including a required 65 percent minimum sensible heat recovery efficiency. Includes plan review and field inspection checklists.

7. [Energy Recovery Ventilators in Cold Climates](https://cchrc.org/wp-content/uploads/media/ERV_ColdClimates.pdf) - Cold Climate Housing Research Center - Reports a monitored winter study of eight flat-plate ERVs in Fairbanks, Alaska. Reviews frost management strategies and finds no frost-related mechanical failures, directly relevant to Minnesota frost and defrost questions.

8. [Energy Recovery in Minnesota: Practical Guide to ERV Operations](https://mn.gov/commerce-stat/pdfs/card-report-cee-erv-practical.pdf) - Center for Energy and Environment and Minnesota Department of Commerce - Draws on field tests of nine Minnesota ERV systems. Explains flow balance, wheel versus fixed-plate types, and why real installations often underperform their expectations.

9. [A Quick Guide to Validate Air-to-Air Exhaust Energy Recovery](https://mn.gov/commerce-stat/pdfs/card-guide-air-exhaust-energy-recovery.pdf) - Minnesota Department of Commerce CARD Program - Short checklist stating that Minnesota ERVs can displace 70 to 80 percent of ventilation load. Shows how to confirm airflow passes the core, how frost control reduces recovery below 0°F, and how unbalanced flows cut savings.

10. [Certified Home Ventilating Products Directory](https://www.hvi.org/) - Home Ventilating Institute - Hosts the third-party certified directory of residential ventilation products, including HRVs and ERVs. Useful for comparing manufacturer-claimed recovery efficiencies, since the test standards and conditions behind published ratings differ between products.
