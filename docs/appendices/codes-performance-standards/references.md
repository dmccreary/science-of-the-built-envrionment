# References: Energy Codes and Building Performance Standards

1. [International Energy Conservation Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Energy_Conservation_Code) - Wikipedia - Describes the IECC, published by the International Code Council and revised on a three-year cycle. Supports this appendix's code edition cycle concept, including how states adopt it as their minimum efficiency requirements.

2. [Building code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_code) - Wikipedia - Explains model codes, which have no legal standing until a jurisdiction adopts them by reference. Supports the appendix's authority chain from model code to local enforcement, noting that most U.S. municipalities adopt rather than write codes.

3. [Energy Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Star) - Wikipedia - Covers the EPA program behind Portfolio Manager, the benchmarking tool that scores commercial buildings from 1 to 100. Useful background for building performance standards, which depend on measured energy data and mandatory benchmarking in several cities.

4. How Buildings Learn: What Happens After They're Built - Stewart Brand - Penguin - Brand adapted Frank Duffy's layers of different longevity into six "shearing layers": site, structure, skin, services, space plan, and stuff. This is the idea behind the appendix's timeline problem and component replacement cycle.

5. Building Codes Illustrated: A Guide to Understanding the 2021 International Building Code (7th Edition) - Francis D. K. Ching and Steven R. Winkel - Wiley - No single originator stands out for energy codes, so this is chosen for clarity: it pairs plain-language code explanations with line drawings, showing how a model code becomes a usable rulebook.

6. [Model Energy Code Determinations](https://www.energycodes.gov/determinations) - U.S. Department of Energy Building Energy Codes Program - Explains how DOE reviews each new ASHRAE 90.1 and IECC edition and requires states to certify code updates. Lists projected national energy savings for recent editions, illustrating the edition cycle.

7. [ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/bookstore/standard-90-1) - ASHRAE - Describes the standard that Minnesota law names for its commercial energy code, covering envelope, mechanical, lighting, and renewable energy provisions. Notes the 2025 edition, built from 105 addenda, and its use as a worldwide code basis.

8. [What Are Building Performance Standards?](https://www.energystar.gov/buildings/resources-topic/what-are-building-performance-standards) - ENERGY STAR, U.S. EPA - Defines building performance standards as a measurable target plus a compliance deadline for existing buildings. Contrasts them with codes for new construction and explains Portfolio Manager's role in measuring and reporting.

9. [Minnesota Advanced Energy Codes Partnership](https://www.mncee.org/minnesota-advanced-energy-codes-partnership) - Center for Energy and Environment - Describes the state-led effort to reach the 80 percent commercial code improvement by 2036 against a 2004 baseline. Features plan review support for cities and the BuildUp MN training hub.

10. [Local Law 97](https://accelerator.nyc/ll97) - NYC Accelerator - Explains New York City's building emissions law, which caps annual carbon emissions for most buildings over 25,000 square feet and fines $268 per excess ton. Includes compliance guides, the 2030 stricter limits, and a free lookup tool.
