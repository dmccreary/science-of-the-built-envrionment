# References: Digital Design, Prefabrication, Robotics, and AI

1. [Building information modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_information_modeling) - Wikipedia - Explains BIM as a shared digital model carrying data on every element. Supports the appendix's BIM and clash detection discussion, with sections on IFC and COBie data standards and levels of development.

2. [Prefabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefabrication) - Wikipedia - Covers factory assembly of components shipped to the site, from history to current practice. Relevant to the appendix's prefabrication section, with advantages, transport and crane drawbacks, and component, panelized, volumetric, and modular categories.

3. [Construction robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_robot) - Wikipedia - Describes industrial robots used on site or in offsite component production. Supports the appendix's construction robotics concept, with capabilities such as inspection, progress monitoring, masonry, earthmoving, and 3D printing, plus labor and worker-safety effects.

4. BIM Handbook: A Guide to Building Information Modeling for Owners, Designers, Engineers, Contractors, and Facility Managers (3rd Edition) - Rafael Sacks, Charles Eastman, Ghang Lee, and Paul Teicholz - Wiley - Eastman, called the "father of BIM," pioneered solid and parametric building modeling. The handbook carries that into vendor-neutral, role-by-role chapters for owners, designers, contractors, and fabricators.

5. Prefab Architecture: A Guide to Modular Design and Construction (1st Edition) - Ryan E. Smith - Wiley - Smith frames off-site construction through a theory of production drawn from lean manufacturing, and weighs scope, schedule, and cost tradeoffs for components, panels, and modules. Clarifies this appendix's prefabrication concerns.

6. [Modular Homes, Modular and Industrialized Buildings](https://www.dli.mn.gov/business/manufactured-structures/modular-homes-modular-and-industrialized-buildings) - Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry - Explains how Minnesota regulates factory-built buildings under Minn. Rules 1361. Shows the code-approval practice the appendix flags, with plan review, labels, factory inspections, and local installation inspections.

7. [Project Overcoat: Investigation of a Process for Affordable High-Performance Enclosure Upgrades for Multifamily Buildings](https://mn.gov/commerce-stat/pdfs/CARD/187556_2023_CSBR_Project-Overcoat_Final.pdf) - Minnesota Department of Commerce CARD, University of Minnesota and Slipstream - Minnesota study of factory-made retrofit wall panels. Ties together prefabrication and reality capture, using a 3D laser scan of the existing building to guide computer-controlled panel manufacturing.

8. [Cost Analysis of Inadequate Interoperability in the U.S. Capital Facilities Industry (NIST GCR 04-867)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/gcr/2004/NIST.GCR.04-867.pdf) - National Institute of Standards and Technology - Estimates $15.8 billion per year in lost efficiency from poor software data exchange, two-thirds borne by owners. Gives the economic case behind BIM and shared models.

9. [Towards an Intelligent Job Site: Status of the NIST Automated Steel Construction Test Bed](https://www.nist.gov/publications/towards-intelligent-job-site-status-nist-automated-steel-construction-test-bed) - National Institute of Standards and Technology - Describes federal research on robotic steel placement, crane automation, laser site measurement, and 3D visualization. Shows construction robotics and reality capture working together toward a 25 percent faster erection goal.

10. [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) - National Institute of Standards and Technology - Presents NIST's voluntary framework for managing AI risk through four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. Supports the appendix's AI output verification theme and its 2024 Generative AI Profile.
