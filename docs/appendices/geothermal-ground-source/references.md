# References: Geothermal Heating and Earth-Coupled Air

1. [Geothermal heat pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_heat_pump) - Wikipedia - Explains ground-source heat pump operation, thermal properties of the ground, and horizontal, vertical, and pond loop arrangements. Supports the appendix's heat pump concept, with typical COP ranges of 3 to 6 and installation and economics sections.

2. [Ground-coupled heat exchanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-coupled_heat_exchanger) - Wikipedia - Describes earth-air tubes buried 1.5 to 3 meters deep, with open, closed, and hybrid configurations. Matches the appendix's earth tube concept and its warnings about condensation drainage, mold, radon, and poorly designed systems.

3. [Seasonal thermal energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_thermal_energy_storage) - Wikipedia - Covers borehole and aquifer storage and district systems that bank summer heat in the ground for winter. Relevant to the appendix's loop-balance and networked geothermal discussion, with examples such as Drake Landing Solar Community.

4. Conduction of Heat in Solids (2nd Edition) - H. S. Carslaw and J. C. Jaeger - Oxford University Press - Carslaw and Jaeger collected exact heat-flow solutions, including a periodic surface temperature in a semi-infinite solid. It gives the damping and delay of seasonal swings with depth behind damping depth, and their cylinder solution underlies loop sizing.

5. Geothermal Heating and Cooling: Design of Ground-Source Heat Pump Systems (1st Edition) - Steve Kavanaugh and Kevin Rafferty - ASHRAE - The Kavanaugh and Rafferty design method, first published in 1997, sizes ground loops with a cylindrical-source equation that treats the changing building load as a series of constant heat-rate pulses. Their book supports the appendix's ground loop and loop-balance concepts.

6. [Geothermal Heating and Cooling Systems](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/water/wells/geothermal.html) - Minnesota Department of Health - Explains how Minnesota regulates closed-loop bored geothermal heat exchangers and open-loop systems. Notes that boreholes are commonly 150 to 200 feet deep, lists approved heat-transfer fluids, and describes the required construction permit.

7. [Minnesota's first NGIA plans bring new electrification and efficiency opportunities](https://www.mncee.org/minnesotas-first-ngia-plans-bring-new-electrification-and-efficiency-opportunities) - Center for Energy and Environment - Reviews Minnesota's Natural Gas Innovation Act plans for Xcel Energy and CenterPoint Energy. Describes Xcel's community ground-source heat pump pilot, a networked geothermal project that prioritizes low-income and environmental justice neighborhoods.

8. [Minnesota Thermal Energy Network Site Suitability Study](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/260051.pdf) - Minnesota Department of Commerce, Division of Energy Resources - A January 2026 report prepared for the Legislature on where thermal energy networks suit Minnesota. Covers ownership models, regulatory frameworks, and a statewide scoring approach, extending the appendix's networked geothermal discussion.

9. [Advanced Testing Method for Ground Thermal Conductivity (ORNL/TM-2017/208)](https://info.ornl.gov/sites/publications/files/Pub74344.pdf) - Oak Ridge National Laboratory - Describes a thermal response test method that finds ground thermal conductivity in 40 to 60 percent less time than the conventional 48-hour test. Shows why designers measure a site's soil rather than assume it.

10. [Collection and Analysis of Soil Thermal Property Data Under Minnesota's Local Conditions](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/978993b6-18c1-4c78-aa21-8557bec951f1/content) - University of Minnesota Duluth undergraduate research project - Compiles soil thermal conductivity, heat capacity, and predominant soil types by Minnesota region. Reports loamy soils in the southwest as most suitable for horizontal loops, supporting the appendix's point that ground performance depends on the site.
