# References: Mass Timber and Low-Carbon Materials

1. [Cross-laminated timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-laminated_timber) - Wikipedia - Describes CLT panels made of crosswise-glued lumber layers, their fire performance, and carbon storage. Useful for this appendix's mass timber concepts, with examples of tall CLT buildings such as Ascent MKE in Milwaukee.

2. [Environmental product declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_product_declaration) - Wikipedia - Explains EPDs as third-party-verified life-cycle reports built on product category rules and ISO standards. Supports the appendix's carbon-counting section, including EN 15804 and ISO 21930 and the difficulty of comparing products.

3. [Embedded emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_emissions) - Wikipedia - Covers greenhouse gases embedded in goods, including the embodied carbon of buildings. Relevant to buy clean procurement, with a section on state procurement policies such as Colorado's law and Toronto's embodied carbon limits.

4. Structural Design for Fire Safety (2nd Edition) - Andrew H. Buchanan and Anthony K. Abu - Wiley - Buchanan, a leading researcher on timber fire behavior, gives timber charring its own chapter section covering charring rate, corner rounding, and the effect of heated wood below the char line. This is the basis for sizing mass timber members.

5. The New Carbon Architecture: Building to Cool the Climate (1st Edition) - Bruce King - New Society Publishers - King reframes embodied carbon as a design target and argues that wood, straw, and bio-based or lower-carbon concrete can make buildings store carbon rather than emit it. This frames the appendix's biogenic carbon and low-carbon materials discussion.

6. [Wood Handbook: Wood as an Engineering Material](https://research.fs.usda.gov/fpl/wood-handbook) - USDA Forest Products Laboratory - The standard U.S. reference on wood properties. Its 2021 revision added CLT content and an updated fire safety chapter that addresses mass timber, plus moisture chapters that support the appendix's charring and moisture protection topics.

7. [DOE Announces Plans To Create Low-Carbon Cement and Concrete Center of Excellence](https://www.energy.gov/eere/iedo/articles/us-department-energy-announces-plans-create-low-carbon-cement-and-concrete) - U.S. Department of Energy - Explains why cement accounts for about 2 percent of U.S. energy-related emissions. Outlines research priorities including testing, modeling, field monitoring, and emissions accounting, which put the appendix's low-carbon concrete strategies in a national context.

8. [Carbon Leadership Forum](https://carbonleadershipforum.org/) - University of Washington - A research and community hub for embodied carbon reduction. Offers a resource library, policy and building-owner toolkits, a state policy playbook, and material baselines, all relevant to EPDs, life-cycle assessment, and buy clean procurement.

9. [Economic Feasibility of Mass Timber Manufacturing in Minnesota](https://lsbe.d.umn.edu/sites/lsbe.d.umn.edu/files/bber-ced_mass_timber_full_report_for_distribution.pdf) - University of Minnesota Duluth Bureau of Business and Economic Research - A Minnesota study of CLT market demand and regional lumber supply. Includes a section on building codes for mass timber and Midwest CLT projects, useful for this appendix's Minnesota perspective.

10. [Minnesota Buy Clean Buy Fair Act: Global Warming Potential Limits](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/2026-MNBuyCleanGWPLimits_2026-01-15_tcm36-724796.pdf) - Minnesota Department of Administration - Sets 2026 maximum global warming potential limits, in kgCO2e per cubic meter, for ready-mix concrete and rebar in state buildings and requires EPD disclosure. A concrete example of buy clean procurement in Minnesota.
