# References: Heat Pumps and Building Electrification

1. [Heat pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump) - Wikipedia - Surveys heat pump operation, types, applications, and performance, including cold-weather behavior and refrigerant choices. Supports this appendix's cold-climate and refrigerant sections, with comparison tables of global warming potential and COP by system type.

2. [Coefficient of performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_performance) - Wikipedia - Defines COP for heating and cooling and derives the theoretical maximum from the temperatures involved. Directly supports the appendix's Carnot limit and lift discussion, with worked ground-source examples and seasonal measures such as SCOP and SEER.

3. [Vapor-compression refrigeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor-compression_refrigeration) - Wikipedia - Explains the four-stage cycle of compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation that every heat pump uses. Matches the appendix's loop description and adds temperature-entropy diagrams, compressor types, and a history of refrigerants from CFCs to HFCs.

4. Sustainable Energy - Without the Hot Air (First Edition, 2008) - David J. C. MacKay - UIT Cambridge - MacKay explains heat pumps by turning a refrigerator inside-out, with the cold box in the garden and the back panel in the kitchen. His Figure E.13 plots ideal heat pump efficiency against temperature, a picture of lift.

5. Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach (9th Edition) - Yunus A. Cengel and Michael A. Boles - McGraw-Hill Education - No single originator stands out for this topic, so this is chosen as a widely used, clearly written text. Its refrigeration cycles chapter defines COP for refrigerators and heat pumps, the reversed Carnot cycle, and the ideal vapor-compression cycle.

6. [How Does a Heat Pump Work?](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ask-the-experts/how-does-heat-pump-work) - ENERGY STAR - Plain-language explanation of how refrigerant pressure changes let a heat pump absorb heat outdoors and release it indoors, then reverse for cooling. Reinforces the appendix's cycle explanation and notes ducted and ductless installation options.

7. [Residential Cold Climate Heat Pump Challenge](https://www.energy.gov/eere/buildings/residential-cold-climate-heat-pump-challenge) - U.S. Department of Energy - Describes the DOE program that pushed manufacturers toward heat pumps with greater capacity and efficiency below 32°F. Lists eight participating manufacturers, which illustrates the appendix's point that cold-climate performance is improving quickly.

8. [Cold Climate Air Source Heat Pump Product List](https://ashp.neep.org/) - Northeast Energy Efficiency Partnerships and AHRI - Searchable database of heat pump models listed to NEEP's cold-climate specification. Shows how a buyer can check a listing instead of trusting a catalog rating, which supports the appendix's advice to verify cold-weather capacity and COP.

9. [Technology Transitions](https://www.epa.gov/climate-hfcs-reduction/technology-transitions) - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Explains the sector-based limits on hydrofluorocarbon refrigerants in air conditioning and heat pumps under the AIM Act, with links to global warming potential references. Primary regulatory source for the appendix's refrigerant section.

10. [Cold-Climate Air Source Heat Pumps](https://www.mncee.org/cold-climate-air-source-heat-pumps) - Center for Energy and Environment - Minnesota field-study results for cold-climate heat pumps in occupied homes, reporting 35 to 50 percent site energy savings and COP by outdoor temperature. Gives students real Minnesota performance data to compare with the Carnot limit.
