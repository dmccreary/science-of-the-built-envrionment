# The Building Systems Map — Image Generation Prompt

Please generate a new image.

## Critical Rule

**This image must contain absolutely no text of any kind.** No title, no headings, no labels, no letters, no numerals, no captions, no arrows with writing, no signs with readable writing, no logos, no watermark. Node names, link names, and the footer message are added later by an interactive HTML overlay driven by a separate data file. Pictograms (a snowflake, a lightning bolt, a flame, a water drop) are allowed because they are shapes, not characters. Dashes and tick marks must carry no numerals. If any lettering or numbers appear anywhere in the picture, regenerate.

## Image Specifications

- **Format:** PNG
- **Dimensions:** 1536 × 1024 px, landscape, 3:2 ratio
- **Background:** Warm cream (#FBF6EC) with a very faint blueprint grid
- **Audience:** College undergraduates in construction, architecture, and building-systems programs; adult but friendly
- **Style:** Clean modern flat-vector technical illustration with soft shading and a consistent line weight, saturated but professional colors, in the same family as a flat cartoon mascot. Technically accurate components. No photorealism, no 3D render look.

## What to Draw

One **network diagram** of a building's systems. **Seven large rounded nodes** sit in a loose ring (an ellipse wider than it is tall). Each node is a rounded rectangle about 13% of the image width and 15% of its height, filled with a pale tint of its accent color, with a thick border in the full color, and holds a small flat-vector pictogram of that system. Between the nodes run **arrows**, and a **highlighted ripple chain** runs along the bottom. Leave generous cream space around every node and arrow so numbered markers and their leader lines can sit cleanly on top.

Reading order is clockwise from the top: Structure, Fire Protection, Electrical, HVAC, Enclosure, Plumbing, Occupants. In the middle of the ring, draw a faint, pale outline of a small house, as a quiet reminder that the seven nodes are parts of one building. Keep a safe margin of about 2% on every side.

**Link strength is shown by line thickness.** The four named arrows are drawn solid in dark slate (#34495E) with an arrowhead and three different weights: the Enclosure to HVAC arrow is the thickest on the poster, the HVAC to Electrical arrow is nearly as thick, and the Structure to Fire Protection and Plumbing to Enclosure arrows are medium. Thickness is a teaching sketch, not measured data. From the Occupants node, six **thin dashed gray spokes** curve across the inside of the ring to every other node, to show that occupants affect everything.

## Layout Notes

- The core idea is that **every system touches every other**. Keep the ring open and evenly spaced so the four named arrows, the dashed occupant spokes, and the ripple chain are all easy to follow.
- Node colors: Structure chestnut #8B5A2B, Enclosure sage green #6B8E4E, HVAC brick red #C95343, Plumbing slate blue #2468A2, Electrical amber #F5A623, Fire Protection safety orange #FF6A13, Occupants teal #1ABC9C. The ripple chain and its glow use violet #8E44AD so it is not confused with any system.
- Arrow weights must visibly differ: Enclosure to HVAC thickest, HVAC to Electrical close behind, the other two medium, occupant spokes thinnest and dashed.
- Keep line weight, corner radius, and upper-left lighting identical across all nodes.
- Keep every marker position listed above on open cream or on a plain arrow, not on top of a small pictogram detail.

## Do Not Include

- Any text, letters, numbers, labels, or captions, including a footer line
- A title, heading, or legend
- Arrows with text or signs with readable words
- Photorealistic people or faces other than Beau
- Brand names or logos
- A watermark or signature
