The ten regions, the clock ring first and then the nine foundations, are:

1. **Where the Day Goes** — How the day is divided between indoors, vehicles, and outdoors.
2. **Ventilation** — Enough outdoor air to dilute odors, chemicals, and carbon dioxide.
3. **Air Quality** — Low-emission materials and removal of pollutant sources.
4. **Thermal Health** — Comfortable, steady temperature and humidity with individual control.
5. **Moisture** — Inspecting for and fixing leaks, condensation, and damp spots.
6. **Dust & Pests** — Cleaning and sealing so allergens and pests do not settle in.
7. **Safety & Security** — Fire safety, carbon monoxide monitoring, and emergency preparedness.
8. **Water Quality** — Safe drinking water, tested at the point of use.
9. **Noise** — Protection from outdoor sound and control of mechanical noise.
10. **Lighting & Views** — Daylight, task lighting, and sightlines to the outdoors.

## Why This Matters

The "90% indoors" figure traces to the National Human Activity Pattern Survey (NHAPS), funded by the U.S. EPA and reported by Klepeis and colleagues (2001). Its respondents, surveyed by telephone diary in 1992 to 1994, spent about 86.9% of their time indoors and about 5.5% in vehicles. That is an average of surveyed U.S. respondents, not a universal constant, so treat it as a strong rule of thumb.

The nine foundations come from the Healthy Buildings program at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. The poster pairs the two ideas: if people live indoors, the building is their habitat. The clock ring is an illustration of the survey averages, and the foundation summaries are paraphrased from Harvard.
