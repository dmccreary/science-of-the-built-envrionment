The eight phases, in order:

1. **Programming** — The owner's needs, budget, and schedule are written down.
2. **Schematic Design** — The architect explores the overall form and layout.
3. **Design Development** — The systems are chosen and sized.
4. **Construction Documents** — The final drawings and specifications are produced and reviewed for the permit.
5. **Bidding and Procurement** — The owner chooses the builder and secures materials.
6. **Construction** — The work is built, observed, and inspected.
7. **Commissioning** — The installed systems are tested against the design intent.
8. **Occupancy and Closeout** — The building is inspected, certified, and handed over.

The poster also sketches two conceptual ideas. The colored lanes show which roles are most active in each phase, and two curves show that the ability to influence cost and performance falls while the cost of a change rises. Neither is a data plot.

## Why This Matters

This poster is a composite of standard concepts. It uses the eight phases that Chapter 2 of this book uses, the electrical designer's coordination role from Chapter 16, and the permit, plan review, and inspection steps from Chapter 17. It is inspired by the integrated team process described by the Whole Building Design Guide, but it is not a published diagram.

The swim lanes and the two curves are conceptual. They show relative emphasis and direction, not measured effort or dollar amounts, and real projects overlap phases and vary by delivery method. The lesson holds anyway: early decisions are made on paper, when a change costs an hour, and late changes are made in finished work.
