## Why This Matters

The stack effect is the vertical air movement caused by the difference in density between warm indoor air and cold outdoor air. Building Science Corporation's BSI-075 describes it: in winter air leaves a heated building through the top and enters through the bottom, with a neutral pressure plane between them, and the flow reverses in cooling seasons. The effect grows with building height and temperature difference.

The same pressure that wastes heat and causes drafts can compromise smoke control in tall buildings, which is why fire engineers care. The smoke panels are a teaching simplification: real smoke movement also depends on the fire's own heat, the building's leaks, and its smoke-control design, which engineers analyze for each building.
