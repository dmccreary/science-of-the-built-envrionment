The six regions are:

1. **Conduction** — Heat passing through a solid by molecular collisions.
2. **Convection** — Heat carried by moving air or water.
3. **Radiation** — Heat travelling as electromagnetic waves, with no material needed.
4. **Thermal Bridges** — Framing and other paths that carry heat around the insulation.
5. **Slab Without a Thermal Break** — A concrete balcony slab that runs unbroken through the wall.
6. **Slab With a Thermal Break** — The same balcony with an insulating layer interrupting the slab.

## Why This Matters

Conduction, convection, and radiation are the standard textbook description of how heat moves, and building science adds the practical consequence: heat takes the easiest path, so an R-value printed on insulation describes the insulation alone and not the whole wall. Studs, slab edges, and gaps let heat go around it. Passive House practice makes thermal-bridge-free detailing one of its five core principles for that reason.

The thermal image is a teaching illustration drawn from the physics described in Chapter 3, not a real scan, and the balcony details are simplified. Actual performance depends on the product, the climate, and the detailing, so use the adopted energy code and the manufacturer's data for real projects.
