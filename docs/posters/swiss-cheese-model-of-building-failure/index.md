The eight regions, from the first defense to the fix:

1. **Design Slice** — Drawings and details that are both compliant and buildable.
2. **Materials Slice** — The right products, delivered in good condition.
3. **Construction Slice** — Work built as designed.
4. **Inspection Slice** — Checks that catch what earlier slices missed.
5. **Maintenance Slice** — Upkeep for the whole life of the building.
6. **Failure Outcome** — What the arrow does when the holes align.
7. **Three Generic Failure Chains** — A roof leak, a deck collapse, and a smoke-spread incident.
8. **Redundancy and Offset Holes** — Independent defenses that do not line up.

## Why This Matters

James Reason, then at the University of Manchester, published the Swiss cheese model in 1990 to explain accidents in complex systems. Each layer of defense has holes, and an accident happens when the holes in several layers line up. Safety engineering, aviation, and healthcare use the model widely.

This poster applies it to buildings. That application is a teaching adaptation, not a published building standard, and the five slices (design, materials, construction, inspection, maintenance) are a choice made for this book. The failure examples are generic composites, not real events. Chapter 21 teaches the same lesson in its failure chains, and Chapter 18 uses the same picture for fire protection layers.
