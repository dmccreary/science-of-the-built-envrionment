The seven regions are:

1. **Minnesota Climate Zones** — Minneapolis is in zone 6A and northern Minnesota in zone 7.
2. **Climate Data to Look Up** — Design temperatures and degree days come from published data, not memory.
3. **Ice Dams** — Seal the ceiling, insulate, then ventilate the roof.
4. **Long Heating Season** — A continuous, airtight, well-insulated wall with warm-side vapor control.
5. **Frost Depth** — Footings below the frozen zone, or frost-protected foundations.
6. **Freeze-Thaw** — Air-entrained concrete, durable masonry, and drainage.
7. **Summer Humidity** — Dehumidification, sealed and insulated surfaces, and a drying path.

## Why This Matters

U.S. climate zones sort places by temperature and moisture so that one set of code tables can serve a whole region. Minnesota spans two of them: zone 6A for most of the state, including Minneapolis, and zone 7 for the northern counties. Because climate fixes what the weather will do to a building, it is the first design input, and each hard question here has a design answer drawn from Chapters 9, 10, 11, and 13.

The poster deliberately prints no energy-code numbers. Insulation values, window ratings, air-leakage limits, and design temperatures depend on the edition of the code in force, so read them from the adopted Minnesota Energy Code and the local building official. Zone assignments are summarized from a Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry code-change proposal and should be confirmed against the adopted rules.
