    Appendices: Topics Changing Fastest¶

    The Physics Stays Put While the Products Move

    Chapters 1 through 21 teach the science that holds still: loads, heat, air, moisture, fire, and the ways buildings fail. These appendices cover the parts of the trade that will not hold still, and each one shows the unchanging physics underneath. Let's build it right!

    The built environment is anything but timeless. Solar panels that were a luxury a generation ago now sit on ordinary roofs. Heat exchangers recover the warmth of the air a building exhales, batteries on a garage wall carry a house through an outage, and pipes buried in the ground borrow its steady temperature. Heat pumps now heat Minnesota buildings in January, and sensors report on a building's air, water, and energy around the clock. New materials, new codes, and new software arrive every year.

    The laws of physics that govern how buildings work do not change. Heat still flows from warm to cold, loads still travel to the ground, and moisture still moves from wet to dry. That is why the main chapters teach the physics and why these appendices are built differently. Each one names what is changing, then shows the unchanging principle that lets you evaluate it, usually with a worked example you can repeat with next year's prices and products.

    Topics Under the Most Rapid Change¶

    Appendix What is changing The physics that stays
    A. Heat Pumps and Building Electrification Cold-climate machines, variable-speed compressors, new refrigerants, incentives Heat moves from cold to hot only with work; COP depends on the lift
    B. Heat Recovery Ventilation and Modern Heat Exchangers Higher-effectiveness cores, energy recovery, defrost strategies Heat flows from the warmer airstream to the cooler one
    C. Solar Photovoltaics Falling costs, changing incentives, net-metering rules, better modules Output follows the sunlight that reaches the panel
    D. Battery Storage and Grid-Interactive Buildings Falling pack prices, LFP chemistry, safety codes, vehicle-to-home Energy (kWh) and power (kW) are different limits
    E. Geothermal Heating and Earth-Coupled Air Ground loops, networked geothermal, earth tubes Soil below about 10 feet holds a nearly steady temperature
    F. Smart Sensors and Building Automation Cheap sensors, connected controls, digital twins, analytics Mass and energy balance govern what the sensors measure
    G. Mass Timber and Low-Carbon Materials Tall-timber code types, low-carbon concrete, EPDs Large timber chars at a steady rate and keeps its core
    H. Energy Codes and Building Performance Standards Three-year code cycles, state targets, performance standards Heat, air, moisture, and fire questions behind every code
    I. Digital Design, Prefabrication, Robotics, and AI BIM, factory-built components, robotic tools, AI assistants A building must still carry its loads and control water, air, and heat

    How Fast Is Each Topic Moving?¶

    Each appendix lists a last_reviewed date in its frontmatter and a "What to Watch" section naming the facts most likely to go stale. Rates of change differ.

    • Very high: heat pumps and refrigerants (A), solar photovoltaics (C), battery storage (D), smart sensors and building automation (F), and digital design, prefabrication, and AI (I). Prices, products, and rules can change within a year.
    • High: heat recovery ventilation (B), geothermal systems (E), mass timber and low-carbon materials (G), and energy codes and performance standards (H). Technology and rules change on a cycle of a few years.

    How to Read an Appendix¶

    1. Start with the unchanging physics and the worked example; they will still be right in ten years.
    2. Read "What Is Changing" as a snapshot, and check the date at the top and the references at the bottom before relying on a number.
    3. Use "What to Watch" as a checklist when you read a news story or a product brochure on the topic.
    4. Follow the "Connects To" links back to the chapters that build the prerequisite understanding.

    Keeping the Appendices Current¶

    A printed textbook goes out of date the day it is printed. This book is built to be updated, and the plan is for intelligent agents to review these appendices on a regular schedule, check each claim against its source, revise what has changed, and update the last_reviewed date, with the author approving the result. Readers can help by reporting out-of-date information on the Contact page.

    Looking for Timeless Patterns¶

    The goal of these appendices, like the rest of the book, is not to give you rules to memorize. Products and codes change, so the rules would be out of date quickly. The goal is to help you recognize the patterns underneath, the ones Christopher Alexander described in A Timeless Way of Building. You can read the story behind that book and the About page for why this book takes that approach.
