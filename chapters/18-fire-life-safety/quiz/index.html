    3. 18. Fire Protection and Life Safety Requirements

    Quiz: Fire Protection and Life Safety Requirements¶

    Test your understanding of the concepts in this chapter with these review questions, one for each of the 11 concepts covered.

    1. A 12,000 ft² story is mainly assembly use. It includes a 1,000 ft² office and a 2,400 ft² educational area. Using the 10 percent accessory threshold, how is the building classified?¶

    1. Both the office and the educational area are accessory, so the building is a single assembly occupancy
    2. Neither is accessory, so the building is classified as a high hazard occupancy requiring special rules
    3. The office is accessory, but the educational area is 20 percent of the story, so it is a mixed occupancy
    4. Only the educational area is accessory, and the office is what makes the building a mixed occupancy
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The office is 1,000 / 12,000 ≈ 8.3 percent, below about 10 percent, so it can be treated as part of the main use. The educational area is 2,400 / 12,000 = 20 percent, so it is not accessory and creates a mixed occupancy, which must be separated by fire-rated construction or designed to meet the strictest requirement. Misclassifying cascades into every later calculation.

    See: Occupancy Classification

    2. How does life safety differ from property protection?¶

    1. Life safety aims to save the building and contents, while property protection aims to give occupants time to escape
    2. Life safety aims to give occupants time to escape, while property protection aims to save the building and contents
    3. Life safety applies only to sprinklers, while property protection applies only to exits
    4. They are the same objective, but life safety applies to new buildings and property protection to old ones
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A building can be a total loss and still have a successful life-safety outcome if every occupant got out. The code concentrates on life safety first, which is why many rules concern escape routes and smoke. Layered protection includes preventing ignition, detecting and alerting, controlling and suppressing, compartmenting, providing escape, and keeping the route usable.

    3. A glulam beam is 6.75 in. wide and exposed on both sides and the bottom. With a charring rate of 1.5 in. per hour, what width remains after one hour?¶

    1. 3.75 in.
    2. 5.25 in.
    3. 6.75 in.
    4. 0.75 in.
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. One hour of charring removes 1.5 in. from each exposed face. Both sides are exposed, so the remaining width is 6.75 − 2 × 1.5 = 3.75 in. Subtracting one face gives 5.25, subtracting nothing gives 6.75, and subtracting twice the rate on each face leaves 0.75. Large timber loses strength by losing section as the char layer insulates the wood beneath it.

    See: Fire-Resistance Ratings

    4. Why is unit equipment, a light with its own battery and charger, a likely choice for a small building like Riverbend?¶

    1. Unit equipment supplies the fire pump and the elevators, so no other backup source is needed at all
    2. Unit equipment restores power within 10 seconds for all loads, so it replaces all backup systems
    3. Unit equipment is required in every building regardless of its size, so its use is always mandatory
    4. Ninety minutes of battery operation for exit signs and a few fixtures is cheap, and it avoids a generator
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Emergency power supplies loads essential to life safety, such as exit signs, egress lighting, and the fire alarm, when the utility fails. The NEC separates emergency systems, which typically restore power in about 10 seconds, from legally required standby systems and optional standby systems. Emergency lighting commonly must last 90 minutes. A larger building with many floors normally needs a central source.

    5. Which description of the five construction types is correct?¶

    1. Type I is wood-frame, Type II is heavy timber, Type III is all steel, Type IV is concrete, and Type V is masonry
    2. Types I through V differ only in the height of the building and not in the materials used in the structure
    3. Types I and II are noncombustible, III has noncombustible walls with combustible framing, IV is heavy timber, V is wood-frame
    4. Type IV is noncombustible steel, Type V is heavy timber, Type III is concrete, and Types I and II are wood frame
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The subtypes A and B indicate protected construction, with fire-resistance-rated structural elements, and unprotected construction, with fewer or no ratings. Noncombustible does not mean fireproof, since unprotected steel loses much of its strength at the temperatures of a developed fire. The type sets the rating each part must have and, with occupancy, limits building height and area.

    Concept Tested: Construction Types

    See: Construction Types

    6. A room has an occupant load of 250 people. Using about 0.2 in. of clear width per person for doors and level routes, what width is needed?¶

    1. 1,250 in.
    2. 50 in.
    3. 5 in.
    4. 25 in.
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Required width equals occupant load times the width factor: 250 × 0.2 = 50 in. For small rooms the minimum number of exits and door width usually control, so the designer provides at least two doors. A means of egress has three parts: the exit access, such as a corridor, the exit, a protected component such as an enclosed stair, and the exit discharge to the public way.

    7. In what order does an automatic transfer switch manage the handover to a generator?¶

    1. It senses loss of utility power, signals the generator to start, moves loads when it reaches speed, and returns them when utility is stable
    2. It starts the generator first, then senses the loss of utility power, returns loads to the utility, and then moves the loads onto the generator
    3. It moves loads to the generator first, signals the generator to start, senses the loss of power, and then shuts the utility feed off
    4. It senses the loss of power, returns the loads to the utility, starts the generator, and then waits for the utility to come back
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A generator is an engine-driven machine that converts fuel into electricity. It takes several seconds to reach speed, which is why the code allows a delay. The set needs ventilation, an exhaust path, fuel for the run time, and regular testing under load, because an engine that has never been run is the engine that fails when needed.

    See: Generators

    8. Why are a UPS and a generator often used together?¶

    1. The UPS gives long duration after a delay of seconds, while the generator has no delay but lasts only minutes
    2. Both start instantly and run for hours, so one is simply a spare for the other in case of failure
    3. The UPS charges the generator's batteries, so the generator can start reliably in cold winter weather
    4. The generator gives long duration after a delay of seconds, while the UPS has no delay but lasts only minutes
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. In a double-conversion UPS the load is always powered through the batteries and an inverter, so transfer is effectively instantaneous. The battery runs for minutes, enough to bridge the gap while a generator starts or to shut down sensitive equipment. Fire alarm control panels contain their own batteries for the same reason. Unit equipment starts instantly and runs about 90 minutes.

    See: Uninterruptible Power Supply

    9. An electrician cuts openings in a rated wall for conduit and leaves the gaps unsealed. What is the consequence?¶

    1. The wall loses its rating wherever openings are unprotected, because a separation works only if it is continuous
    2. The wall's rating increases, because the conduit adds noncombustible material to the thickness of the wall
    3. The wall keeps its rating, because only doors and ducts can reduce the rating of a rated separation
    4. The wall's rating transfers to the conduit, so the conduit becomes the rated element instead of the wall
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Fire separations are rated walls, floors, and assemblies that divide a building into compartments. The IBC uses fire walls, fire barriers, fire partitions, and smoke barriers. Doors must be rated and self-closing, ducts carry fire dampers, and gaps around pipes and conduit are sealed with tested firestopping. Coordinating before rough-in and marking rated walls on drawings helps inspectors see a sealed system.

    10. Why does the code limit travel distance and measure it along the actual route and not in a straight line?¶

    1. The straight-line distance is always longer than the route, so a limit measured that way would be too generous
    2. The time to walk out is part of the required safe egress time and grows with distance, and routes bend
    3. Travel distance applies only to buildings with sprinklers, so a straight line is easier to measure
    4. The route is measured only in corridors, since rooms themselves have no distance limit for travel
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Travel distance is the maximum path an occupant must follow from the most remote point in a room to the nearest exit. Limits vary by occupancy and are typically longer in sprinklered buildings. In the Riverbend example, the straight diagonal is about 141.5 ft, but the real route to an exit was 160 ft against an illustrative 200 ft limit, and adding an exit door would give more margin.

    11. A design's programmed floor area exceeds the tabulated area for its occupancy and construction type. Which set of responses is correct?¶

    1. Remove all of the exits so the occupant load falls, then apply for a variance from the building official
    2. Reclassify the building as a high hazard occupancy so that the tabulated area automatically increases
    3. Reduce the occupant load factor by half, then add a smoke barrier to the center of each floor
    4. Change the construction type, add sprinklers for an increase, or divide the building with a fire wall
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Allowable building area is the largest floor area the code permits on a single floor, set by combination of occupancy group and construction type. A bigger compartment holds more fuel and more people, so it needs better construction. Sprinklers and open frontage can each raise the tabulated area, and a fire wall makes the two sides separate buildings. Finding an area problem during construction documents can force a change in construction type.
