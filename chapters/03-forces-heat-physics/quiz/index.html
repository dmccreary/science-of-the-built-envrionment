    Quiz: Forces, Heat, and the Physics of Buildings¶

    Test your understanding of the concepts in this chapter with these review questions, one for each of the 16 concepts covered.

    1. A brace carries a 2,000 lb force acting at 30 degrees from the horizontal. What is the vertical component of that force?¶

    1. 1,732 lb
    2. 2,000 lb
    3. 1,000 lb
    4. 2,732 lb
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The vertical component is F sin θ = 2,000 × sin 30° = 1,000 lb. The value 1,732 lb is the horizontal component (F cos θ), 2,000 lb treats the whole force as vertical, and 2,732 lb adds the two components as if they were collinear. Components always combine as the sides of a right triangle, not as a simple sum.

    2. How does heat differ from temperature?¶

    1. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference, while temperature is average molecular motion
    2. Heat is the average motion of molecules in a material, while temperature is the energy transferred between objects
    3. Heat is measured in degrees, while temperature is measured in BTU or joules
    4. Heat and temperature are the same quantity expressed in different unit systems
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A bathtub of lukewarm water contains much more heat than a sparkler's spark, though the spark is far hotter. Temperature describes how fast molecules move on average, whereas heat is energy in transit between objects at different temperatures. Heat is measured in BTU or joules, temperature in degrees, so the two cannot be interchanged.

    3. Why is it more accurate to say a heated building loses heat in winter than to say cold enters the building?¶

    1. Cold is a form of energy that moves from outdoors into the walls, but only through windows and doors
    2. Heat always flows from warmer to colder regions, so heat leaves through walls, roof, windows, and floor
    3. Heat flows toward the region with more air, so the warm interior draws in the colder outdoor air
    4. Heat flows only through conduction, so cold cannot cross the enclosure by other means
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Heat transfer is the movement of heat from higher to lower temperature, and the rate increases with the temperature difference. A draft is warm air leaving and replacement air arriving that the furnace must heat. Cold is not a thing that moves, and heat moves by conduction, convection, and radiation, not conduction alone.

    4. A 20 ft beam rests on supports A and B at its ends. A 4,000 lb point load sits 5 ft from A. What is the reaction at A?¶

    1. 1,000 lb
    2. 2,000 lb
    3. 4,000 lb
    4. 3,000 lb
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Take moments about B: R_A × 20 = 4,000 × 15, so R_A = 3,000 lb. The reaction at B is 1,000 lb, and the two reactions sum to the 4,000 lb load. The support nearer the load carries more of it. Choosing 2,000 lb assumes the load is centered, and 4,000 lb assigns the whole load to one support.

    5. Why does a footing under a post reduce the risk of overloading the soil?¶

    1. It reduces the weight of the post so less force reaches the soil
    2. It spreads the same force over a larger area, so the stress on the soil is lower
    3. It converts compressive stress in the post into tensile stress in the soil
    4. It increases the strain in the soil so the soil carries more total force
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Stress equals force divided by area. A 6,000 lb post load on a 2 ft by 2 ft footing is 1,500 psf, far lower than the stress on the post itself. Materials fail when stress at a location reaches their limit, so enlarging the area keeps the soil below its allowable pressure. The footing does not change the load or convert stress types.

    See: Stress

    6. Through which materials is conduction the main mechanism of heat transfer?¶

    1. Moving fluids such as warm air rising from a radiator or water circulating in a pipe
    2. Empty space between the sun and a window surface
    3. Solid building materials such as wood, concrete, steel, and insulation
    4. Open air gaps and cavities where warm and cool air circulate freely by buoyancy
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Conduction passes heat by collisions between neighboring molecules without bulk motion of the material, so it dominates in solids. Moving fluids transfer heat by convection, and empty space carries heat by radiation. Air gaps involve convection and radiation. Insulation works because trapped still air limits conduction through a mostly gas-filled material.

    See: Conduction

    7. A wall has several layers in series between the warm room and the cold outdoors. How do their thermal resistances combine?¶

    1. They average, and the temperature drop divides equally among the layers
    2. They multiply, and the thinnest layer takes the largest share of the temperature drop
    3. They add, and the layer with the lowest resistance takes the largest share of the temperature drop across the wall
    4. They add, and the layer with the highest resistance takes the largest share of the temperature drop
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Thermal resistance follows Ohm's law in a thermal costume: layers in series add, like resistors in series. The temperature difference divides in proportion to each layer's resistance, so in the example about 67 of the 80°F drop falls across the insulation. Averaging or multiplying resistances misrepresents series heat flow, and the low-resistance layers take the smallest share.

    8. A designer needs R-20 of roof insulation. How thick must the layer be if polyisocyanurate board provides R-5 per inch?¶

    1. 4 in
    2. 0.25 in
    3. 15 in
    4. 100 in
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The thickness equals the target R divided by the R per inch: 20 / 5 = 4 in. The value 0.25 in inverts the ratio, 15 in subtracts instead of dividing, and 100 in multiplies. A higher R-value means more resistance, so reaching the target with a material that has more R per inch needs a thinner layer, which matters when the roof is shallow.

    9. Why does a person standing near a large window on a winter day feel chilled even when the thermostat reads 70°F?¶

    1. Cold air flows through the glass by conduction and replaces the warm air near the skin and face
    2. The skin radiates heat toward the colder window surface, and that net radiant loss feels like a chill
    3. The window emits cold radiation that is absorbed by the skin
    4. The thermostat is measuring convection, which is unrelated to how warm a surface feels
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Every object above absolute zero emits thermal radiation, and a colder surface returns less than it receives. The worked example shows net exchange to a 40°F pane is about double that to a 68°F wall. Cold is not emitted, and the sensation does not depend on air temperature alone, which is why low-emissivity coatings and warmer inner glass surfaces improve comfort.

    Concept Tested: Radiation

    See: Radiation

    10. Which statement correctly describes strain?¶

    1. It is the internal force divided by the cross-sectional area, in psi
    2. It is the permanent deformation that remains after any stress is applied
    3. It is the change in length divided by the original length, and it has no units
    4. It is the force applied to a member divided by the change in its length
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Strain is ε = ΔL / L, a dimensionless ratio because the two lengths cancel, so 0.001 means one thousandth of the length. The force-over-area quantity is stress, not strain. Permanent deformation describes plastic behavior after the elastic limit is exceeded, and strain by itself says nothing about force divided by deflection.

    11. A batt labeled R-13 sits between wood studs, yet the framed wall performs well below R-13. What best explains the gap?¶

    1. Insulation loses its R-value whenever it touches a stud, so the cavity is effectively uninsulated
    2. Studs add resistance in parallel, so the total resistance is the sum of the stud and cavity paths
    3. The R-value on the label includes only the siding and sheathing, not the insulation
    4. Studs conduct heat more easily than insulation and bypass the cavity, so the effective R is lower
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Heat chooses the easiest path. Studs make up roughly a quarter of a wall's area, and the stud path totals about R-6.8 versus R-15.4 at the cavity, so the area-weighted U-value gives an effective R near 11.7. The label describes the insulation alone, and parallel paths are combined by averaging U-values, not by adding resistances.

    See: Thermal Bridging

    12. What distinguishes forced convection from natural convection?¶

    1. In forced convection a fan, pump, or wind drives the fluid, while in natural convection density drives it
    2. In forced convection heat travels through solids, while in natural convection it travels through gases
    3. In forced convection the fluid is still, while in natural convection it moves in layers
    4. In forced convection electromagnetic waves carry the heat, while in natural convection molecules collide
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Convection is heat transfer by the motion of a fluid. In natural convection, warm fluid is lighter and rises while cool fluid sinks; in forced convection an external agent such as a fan, pump, or wind drives the motion. Heat through solids is conduction and heat by electromagnetic waves is radiation. A moving fluid exchanges heat faster than still air.

    13. How is the U-value of an assembly related to its R-value?¶

    1. It equals the total R-value multiplied by the area
    2. It equals the reciprocal of the assembly's total R-value
    3. It equals the sum of the R-values of each layer
    4. It equals the R-value divided by the temperature difference
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The U-value is the rate of heat flow per square foot per degree, so U = 1 / R_total, with the total including every layer and the air films. Heat loss then equals U × area × ΔT. Summing layer R-values gives the total R, not U, and multiplying by area or dividing by ΔT does not produce the reciprocal.

    14. Why does a fixed overhang on a south window block summer heat but admit winter sun in Minneapolis?¶

    1. The noon sun is much higher in summer than in winter, so the overhang shades more glass in summer
    2. The overhang moves with the seasons so that it covers the window only when the sun is highest
    3. The summer sun shines from the north side, so the south window needs shading in winter
    4. The overhang reflects infrared radiation in summer and transmits it in winter
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. At about 45° north latitude the noon sun altitude is roughly 68° in June and 22° in December. A 2 ft overhang shades about 81 percent of a 5 ft window in summer but leaves it fully in sun in winter. The overhang is fixed with no moving parts and does not filter radiation by season, and the sun does not shine from the north in summer at noon.

    15. Which property best describes thermal mass?¶

    1. A material's resistance to heat flow, determined by its thickness divided by its conductivity
    2. A material's ability to emit radiation, determined by its emissivity from zero to one
    3. A material's weight per cubic foot, determined by the load it places on the foundation
    4. A material's capacity to store heat and release it slowly, determined by its mass and specific heat
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Heavy materials with high specific heat, such as water, concrete, and masonry, absorb much heat per degree and so warm and cool slowly. Resistance to heat flow is thermal resistance, and emissivity describes radiation. Thermal mass delays heat flow but does not stop it, so it cannot substitute for insulation.

    16. Which rating describes a wall or floor's resistance to airborne sound?¶

    1. Impact Insulation Class (IIC)
    2. Decibel Absorption Rating (DAR)
    3. Sound Transmission Class (STC)
    4. Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC)
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Airborne sound such as speech is rated by the Sound Transmission Class, and codes commonly require about STC 50 between dwelling units. The Impact Insulation Class rates resistance to impact sound such as footsteps. Higher numbers are better for both. The other two options are not the building-science ratings for airborne sound through separating walls and floors in this chapter.
