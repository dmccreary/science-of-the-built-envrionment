    3. 21. Durability, Maintenance, and Building Failure

    Quiz: Durability, Maintenance, and Building Failure¶

    Test your understanding of the concepts in this chapter with these review questions, one for each of the 20 concepts covered.

    1. Why is it incorrect to call a material durable in general?¶

    1. Durability is the same as strength, so any strong material is durable in every environment and every climate zone
    2. Durability depends only on cost, so an expensive material is durable in any environment and under any exposure conditions
    3. Durability applies only to metals and coatings, so other materials cannot be described as durable in any climate
    4. Durability is performance in a specific environment, so brick lasting centuries in a dry climate may crumble in freeze-thaw
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Durability is the ability to keep performing a required function in the service environment with only planned maintenance. It is not the same as strength, since a strong material can still decay if it stays wet, and it has a cost dimension because a material that needs frequent expensive repair is not durable in any useful sense. Water is the most important agent of degradation, followed by temperature swings, sunlight, chemicals, organisms, wear, and fire.

    2. A stain on a ceiling traces back to a missing flashing detail on the drawings. What does this illustrate about how building failures occur?¶

    1. Failures usually result from a chain of conditions, so a stain may begin on a drawing, and breaking any link prevents loss
    2. Failures have a single cause, which is always found at the location of the visible symptom in the building
    3. Failures occur only through collapse of the structure, so a stain on a ceiling is not a failure of any kind
    4. Failures are caused by environmental loads alone, so the drawings and the construction cannot contribute to them
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Building failure is the condition in which a building or part no longer meets its required performance, from rare collapse to common serviceability failures such as leaks, cracks, and mold. The cause categories are design, materials, construction, maintenance, environment, and use. In the flashing example, the detail was omitted, installers worked from the drawings, wind-driven rain entered the wall, and decay followed over several years.

    3. Why does a forensic investigator propose several competing hypotheses and protect the evidence before repairs begin?¶

    1. Only one hypothesis can be tested at a time, so the investigator must pick the cheapest one to test first
    2. The evidence is created by the repair itself, so repairs should be completed before any investigation can begin
    3. Each hypothesis is tested against the evidence, and repairing the failed part first could destroy evidence of the cause
    4. Hypotheses are written for legal reasons only, so the testing of each one against the evidence is optional
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Building forensics investigates what happened, why, and who or what was responsible, unlike a building assessment, which describes condition. The steps are to define the problem and protect the evidence, collect facts, propose competing hypotheses, test each by observation, sampling, laboratory work, analysis, or a water test, and write a conclusion. In the Riverbend example, a bottom-up water test leaked only at the window head.

    4. A component has a reference service life of 30 years. Using the factor method, the design factor is 0.9, outdoor environment 0.8, and maintenance 1.1, with all other factors 1.0. What is the estimated service life?¶

    1. About 21.6 years
    2. About 23.8 years
    3. About 30 years
    4. About 33 years
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The factor method multiplies the reference life by every factor: 30 × 0.9 × 0.8 × 1.1 ≈ 23.8 years. Leaving out the maintenance factor gives 21.6, and ignoring all factors returns the reference life. Factors below 1.0 shorten life and above 1.0 lengthen it. The result shows which decisions move the number, such as improving drainage details or skipping maintenance, and it lets the owner schedule a replacement fund.

    See: Service Life

    5. A condition assessment finds $450,000 of needed repairs, and the replacement value is $6,000,000. What is the facility condition index, and how is it commonly described?¶

    1. 7.5 percent, commonly described as fair
    2. 7.5 percent, commonly described as good
    3. 13.3 percent, commonly described as poor
    4. 0.75 percent, commonly described as good
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The FCI equals the cost of needed repairs divided by current replacement value: 450,000 / 6,000,000 = 0.075, or 7.5 percent. Owners commonly describe about 5 percent or less as good, 5 to 10 percent as fair, and above that as poor, though bands differ among organizations. Assessments combine record review, interviews, visual walk-throughs, non-destructive tools such as moisture meters and infrared cameras, and destructive investigation where needed.

    6. What are the four strategies for controlling water intrusion, sometimes called the four Ds?¶

    1. Design, demolish, dehumidify, and discard the wet materials
    2. Dig, dam, dilute, and divert groundwater away
    3. Detect, document, defer, and dispute claims
    4. Deflect, drain, dry, and use durable materials
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Water intrusion is the unwanted entry of liquid water and the most common cause of enclosure failures. Sources include rain driven through gaps, roof leaks, ice dams, groundwater, plumbing leaks, and capillary rise. Deflecting uses overhangs, flashings, and slopes; draining uses a drainage plane; drying lets vapor escape; and durable materials are used where wetting is unavoidable. Finding the point of entry can be difficult.

    7. How does a retrofit differ from a renovation, and what makes existing buildings harder to work on than new ones?¶

    1. A retrofit replaces the entire building with a new one, while existing buildings have no hidden conditions at all
    2. A retrofit adds new components to improve specific performance, while hidden conditions and hazardous materials add uncertainty
    3. A retrofit applies only to new buildings under construction, while existing buildings are exempt from the code
    4. A retrofit removes all hazardous materials from a building, while a renovation never encounters any of them
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A renovation alters, repairs, or upgrades a building to extend its life or improve performance, while a retrofit adds new components, such as attic insulation, air sealing, new windows, and a heat pump, to improve energy, safety, or accessibility. Codes treat existing buildings through alteration provisions with requirements that scale with the extent of the work, so the team confirms with the building official.

    Concept Tested: Renovation and Retrofit

    See: Renovation and Retrofit

    8. Vibration monitoring of a motor, which triggers servicing when measurements show a failure approaching, is an example of which maintenance style?¶

    1. Reactive maintenance
    2. Preventive maintenance
    3. Predictive maintenance
    4. Deferred maintenance
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Maintenance has three styles. Reactive maintenance fixes something after it fails, costs nothing until the failure, but failures occur at the worst times and can damage other parts. Preventive maintenance services components on a schedule to avoid failure. Predictive maintenance services when measurements show a failure approaching. A well-run building uses all three, and the owner budgets for them at the outset.

    9. Option 1 costs $6 per ft² and lasts 15 years, and Option 2 costs $12 per ft² and lasts 30 years. Both total $24 per ft² undiscounted over 60 years. At a 3 percent discount rate, which has the lower present value?¶

    1. Option 2, because it lasts longer and so always has the lower present value at any discount rate
    2. Option 1, because it spreads its spending over time and later costs are worth less today
    3. They tie, because discounting affects both options equally when the totals are the same undiscounted
    4. Neither can be determined without knowing the initial cost of the maintenance over the full study period
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Life-cycle cost converts future costs to present value with PV = cost / (1 + r)ⁿ. Option 1 is installed in years 0, 15, 30, and 45, giving about 6 + 3.85 + 2.47 + 1.59 ≈ $13.91 per ft². Option 2 is installed in years 0 and 30, giving 12 + 4.94 ≈ $16.94. The discount rate matters because a larger first cost is not discounted at all.

    Concept Tested: Life-Cycle Cost

    See: Life-Cycle Cost

    10. What is passive survivability, and why does it matter in a Minnesota January?¶

    1. How long a building's roof lasts without any maintenance, and a snow-covered roof is stronger because of the added weight
    2. How quickly occupants can evacuate a building in a fire, and a cold building has fewer exits that can be used
    3. How long a generator can run on one tank of fuel, and a cold building uses less fuel while it is running
    4. How long a building stays livable without power or fuel, and an insulated, airtight building loses heat slowly, delaying frozen pipes
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Building resilience is the ability to withstand, adapt to, and recover quickly from disruptive events, such as severe storms, flooding, extreme heat or cold, and loss of power or water. It treats rare severe events as design inputs. Examples include placing equipment above flood levels, providing emergency power for critical functions, using shading and insulation to limit temperature swings, and arranging systems so one failure does not disable the others.

    11. Why is a preventive task such as clearing gutters in spring and fall considered economical?¶

    1. A small planned task costs far less than the water damage that follows if it is neglected
    2. It costs more than the repairs it avoids, but it is required by the building code in every jurisdiction
    3. It replaces the need for flashing, so the roof can be built more cheaply by omitting the metal flashings
    4. It prevents all failures from ever occurring, so no other kind of maintenance is needed in the building
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Preventive maintenance is scheduled servicing and inspection at set intervals to keep components working and to find small problems before they become large ones. The schedule follows manufacturer recommendations, building exposure, and history. A typical schedule includes roof, flashing, and drain inspections twice a year, sealant checks yearly, HVAC filter replacement often quarterly, and testing emergency lights and the fire alarm.

    12. A 50 ft (600 in.) concrete wall with α = 5.5 × 10⁻⁶ per °F experiences a 100°F seasonal temperature swing. How much does its length change if it is free to move?¶

    1. About 0.033 in.
    2. About 3.3 in.
    3. About 0.33 in.
    4. About 0.66 in.
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Thermal movement follows ΔL = α L ΔT = 5.5 × 10⁻⁶ × 600 × 100 = 0.33 in. Using 0.66 in. would apply to a 100 ft wall, and the others are decimal slips. This explains why designers place control joints and expansion joints, which give a material a planned place to move. A wall without joints will find its own place to crack, and width, direction, and pattern point to the cause.

    13. Two columns 30 ft (360 in.) apart settle differently by 0.5 in. Compared with a rule-of-thumb limit of 1/500 (0.0020), what is the angular distortion and what does it suggest?¶

    1. About 0.0014, above the limit of 0.0020, so cracking is expected in the walls
    2. About 0.72, far above the limit, so collapse of the frame is expected
    3. About 0.0028, above the limit of 0.0020, so cracking is expected in the walls
    4. About 0.0014, below the limit, so cracking is not expected from the rule of thumb
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Angular distortion is differential settlement divided by the distance between the points: 0.5 / 360 ≈ 0.0014, or about 1/720, which is below 1/500. The rule of thumb is not a code limit, and the foundation engineer designs to actual criteria. Uniform settlement is usually harmless, while differential settlement distorts the frame and causes cracks, sticking doors, and sloping floors.

    14. Why does dry wood last for centuries even though the wood is a food source for decay fungi?¶

    1. Fungi need only moisture to grow, and wood always contains too little of it unless it is submerged in water
    2. Fungi need only a food source, and dry wood is not food for them because its cell walls are sealed
    3. Fungi need food, moisture above about 20 percent, oxygen, and moderate temperature at once, and removing one stops growth
    4. Fungi cannot live in wood above 40°F, so dry wood is always too cold for the fungi to establish growth
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Decay fungi turn wood into a crumbly, weak mass, and wood-boring insects such as carpenter ants and termites also damage wet wood. The remedies follow from the four needs: keep wood dry through detailing, separate it from soil and concrete, use preservative-treated or naturally durable wood where wetting is expected, and provide a drying path. The steel counterpart is corrosion.

    15. What should be done first when mold is found growing on a damp wall?¶

    1. Correct the moisture source, then dry the area and remove or clean the affected materials
    2. Paint over the mold so that the spores cannot spread to other surfaces in the building
    3. Bleach the surface and leave the moisture source in place until the next season
    4. Seal the area with an air-tight membrane until it dries out by itself over a long time
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Mold is a fungus that grows on damp organic surfaces, such as the paper facing on gypsum board, dust, or wood, and it can start within a couple of days of a material becoming wet. Condensation on a cold surface, a leak, or a flood can supply the moisture. Wiping a stain without fixing the leak only hides the problem, and the damage keeps growing out of sight.

    16. A white powdery band appears along the base of a brick wall. Brushing removes it, but it returns. What does this indicate?¶

    1. Mold is growing on the brick surface, so the wall must be treated with a fungicide and then sealed again
    2. Water is moving through the wall and carrying salts to the surface, so a leak or drainage problem should be investigated
    3. The brick is deteriorating from sunlight, so it must be coated with a protective sealer on all of its faces
    4. The salts come only from the cleaning product used on the wall, so no investigation of the wall is needed
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Efflorescence forms when water dissolves soluble salts and carries them to the surface, where the water evaporates and leaves the salts behind. It is not mold and does not damage the material by itself. It is a signal that water is passing through the wall. Washing removes the deposit but it returns if the water source remains.

    17. Why is air-entrained concrete the standard defense against freeze-thaw damage for exterior concrete in cold climates?¶

    1. Microscopic bubbles give freezing water, which expands about 9 percent, room to expand without cracking the concrete
    2. Microscopic bubbles stop all water from ever entering the concrete, so that freezing never occurs inside it
    3. Microscopic bubbles increase the compressive strength of the concrete to well above 10,000 psi in all cases
    4. Microscopic bubbles make deicing salts harmless, so the salts can be used freely on young concrete surfaces
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Freeze-thaw damage is the cracking, flaking, and crumbling of porous materials such as concrete, brick, and stone caused by water freezing inside them. It appears as spalling and as scaling, which deicing salts make worse. A low water-to-cement ratio also makes concrete less permeable, brick is selected for freeze-thaw resistance, and flashings, copings, and drainage keep water out of masonry.

    See: Freeze-Thaw Damage

    18. After a fire, a steel beam looks sound and unwarped. What should happen before the building is reoccupied?¶

    1. The beam should be accepted as sound, because steel is a noncombustible material that cannot be harmed by heat
    2. A structural engineer should assess every damaged member, because a member can look sound yet have lost strength
    3. The beam should be painted and reoccupied, because the damage from the fire is only cosmetic in appearance
    4. The beam should be tested only if it is wood, because only wood chars and loses section in a fire
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Fire damage is the harm done by heat, flame, smoke, and the water used to extinguish a fire. Steel loses roughly half its strength near 1,100°F, concrete loses strength and may spall, and wood chars on the surface while the core remains sound. Smoke and soot spread beyond the burned area, and firefighting water often causes more widespread damage than the flames. Repairs trigger permits and inspections.

    19. Why does converting a warehouse into apartments often trigger major code upgrades?¶

    1. A new use never changes the code requirements, because the building already exists and was previously approved
    2. A new use removes the need for a certificate of occupancy, because the structure has already been inspected
    3. A new use changes the occupancy classification, bringing new requirements for fire separations, egress, and sprinklers
    4. A new use applies only to the roof and has no effect on any of the other parts of the building
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Adaptive reuse is the conversion of an existing building to a use other than the one for which it was built, such as a flour mill that becomes housing or a museum. It saves the embodied carbon of the existing structure, preserves a community's character, and may qualify for historic preservation incentives. A feasibility study tests the structure, the code path, and the cost before the owner commits.

    See: Adaptive Reuse

    20. How does deconstruction differ from demolition, and what makes it competitive?¶

    1. Fast, mechanical destruction of the building so the debris can be hauled away, competing through lower labor cost
    2. Conversion of a building to a new use while keeping the structure, competing through historic preservation incentives
    3. Installation of new systems in an existing building to improve its performance, competing through energy savings
    4. Careful, selective dismantling to recover materials for reuse, competing through avoided disposal costs and resale value
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Deconstruction proceeds in roughly the reverse order of construction, with crews removing fixtures, doors, windows, flooring, and structural timber and sorting them for resale, donation, or recycling. It takes more labor than demolition and needs a hazardous-material survey before work. It works best when a building was designed for it, with mechanical fasteners and accessible connections, which is why designers who think about reuse at the start make it easier.
