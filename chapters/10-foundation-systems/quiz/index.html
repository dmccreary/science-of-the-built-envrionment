    Quiz: Foundation Systems¶

    Test your understanding of the concepts in this chapter with these review questions, one for each of the 15 concepts covered.

    1. A geotechnical report finds 15 ft of soft peat above firm sand beneath a light building. Which foundation recommendation follows the chapter's logic?¶

    1. Spread footings bearing on the peat at the frost depth
    2. Piles that pass through the peat to bear in the firm sand
    3. Wider continuous footings placed on the peat surface
    4. A thicker slab-on-grade placed directly on the peat
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A foundation spreads load over enough soil that the soil is not overstressed. Designers begin with the cheapest option that works, almost always shallow, and move deeper only when soil forces them to. Shallow footings on 15 ft of soft peat would settle badly, so deep foundations that reach firm soil below the weak layer are needed. The decision follows the soil.

    2. Why does a "shallow" exterior footing in Minneapolis still sit more than 3 ft below the surface?¶

    1. Shallow describes the depth of the concrete, and Minnesota soil requires a minimum of 3 ft for all footings
    2. Shallow footings must reach the water table so that groundwater supports the building
    3. Shallow footings must sit below the topsoil, which is always at least 3 ft deep
    4. Shallow describes how load reaches the soil, and the bottom must sit below frost depth, commonly 42 in
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Shallow foundations carry load to soil near the surface through a footing wider than the wall or column above it. The word does not refer to the concrete's depth, and frost depth sets it for exterior footings. Frost depth governs the depth, and bearing capacity governs the width. An interior footing in a heated building may not need the frost rule.

    3. A bearing wall delivers 3,000 plf to soil with an allowable bearing capacity of 2,000 psf. What footing width is required?¶

    1. 18 in
    2. 8 in
    3. 36 in
    4. 1.5 in
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The required width equals line load divided by allowable pressure: 3,000 / 2,000 = 1.5 ft, which is 18 in. Dividing the other way gives 0.67 ft (8 in), 36 in doubles the answer, and 1.5 in confuses feet with inches. Footing thickness is set separately, commonly 8 in or more for a light-frame building, so the footing is stiff enough to spread the load without cracking.

    4. A 9 ft basement wall retains soil with an equivalent fluid pressure of 40 pcf. What is the lateral force per foot of wall?¶

    1. 360 lb per foot
    2. 3,240 lb per foot
    3. 1,620 lb per foot
    4. 540 lb per foot
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The force is the area of the pressure triangle: F = ½ γ_e h² = 0.5 × 40 × 81 = 1,620 lb per foot, acting one third of the height above the base. The value 360 psf is only the pressure at the base, 3,240 omits the one-half, and 540 mixes up the height. A foundation wall carries the vertical load of the wall above and also resists this lateral pressure.

    5. In what two ways does a deep foundation element resist load?¶

    1. Footing spread at the base and mat action across the top
    2. Hydrostatic uplift and perimeter drainage of the walls
    3. End bearing at the tip and skin friction along the sides
    4. Frost heave and lateral soil pressure on the walls
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Deep foundations pass through weak layers to firmer soil or rock. End bearing is the load carried by the tip pressing on firm soil or rock, and skin friction is the load carried by friction along the sides. A pile cap, a concrete block over a group of piles, ties them together and receives the column load. The other pairs describe shallow foundations or soil forces.

    See: Deep Foundations

    6. A square spread footing 5 ft on a side carries a 45 kip column load. What average pressure does it place on the soil?¶

    1. 9,000 psf
    2. 1,800 psf
    3. 225 psf
    4. 11,250 psf
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Pressure equals load divided by bearing area: 45,000 lb / (5 ft × 5 ft) = 45,000 / 25 = 1,800 psf. Dividing by the 5 ft side alone gives 9,000, 225 divides by 200 ft², and 11,250 divides by only 4 ft². Spread footings are common under steel columns and under posts that support glulam beams.

    7. Why are basements common in cold climates?¶

    1. Footings must go deep for frost anyway, so the added excavation yields usable space cheaply
    2. Basements are required by code in any region where the ground freezes to a depth of 3 ft
    3. Basements eliminate the groundwater and radon problems that slab floors face in cold regions
    4. Basements allow the footings to be placed above the frost depth, saving excavation and concrete
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A basement is a story partly or fully below grade, enclosed by foundation walls. It adds living or storage space, houses mechanical equipment, and keeps the floor above cold ground. It also takes on groundwater, radon, and moisture problems, and habitable basement rooms need an emergency escape opening. Footings still must reach below frost depth.

    8. From the bottom up, what is the typical order of layers in a slab-on-grade?¶

    1. Concrete slab, vapor retarder, granular base, compacted subgrade
    2. Granular base, compacted subgrade, concrete slab, vapor retarder
    3. Compacted subgrade, vapor retarder, concrete slab, granular base
    4. Compacted subgrade, granular base, vapor retarder, concrete slab
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A slab-on-grade is the most economical floor for a building without a basement. The native soil is compacted after topsoil is removed, a granular base gives uniform support and breaks capillary rise, a polyethylene vapor retarder slows moisture moving up into the slab, and the concrete is commonly at least 4 in thick. In cold climates the edge is insulated.

    9. Why is footing reinforcement placed near the bottom of a footing, with 3 in of cover where concrete is cast against earth?¶

    1. The footing bends like a simple beam, so the bottom is in compression, and cover keeps the soil from freezing
    2. The footing bends like an upside-down cantilever, so the bottom is in tension, and cover protects the steel
    3. The footing is loaded in tension by the column above, so the bars hold the column in place at the base
    4. The footing is cracked by frost heave every winter, so the bars keep moisture out of the cracks
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Concrete is strong in compression and weak in tension. A footing loaded from above and pushed up by soil bends so that its bottom face is stretched, and bars are placed there. Spread footings usually contain a two-way grid, and light continuous footings often use a pair of continuous longitudinal bars for crack control. Cover protects steel from corrosion.

    10. Under what circumstances is a mat foundation chosen?¶

    1. When soil is weak, column loads are heavy, or individual footings would nearly touch
    2. When the soil is firm gravel and the building has only a few light columns to support
    3. When the building has no floor, so a continuous footing under each wall is sufficient
    4. When a basement is not allowed, so a thin slab replaces all of the footings
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A mat, or raft, is a thick continuous slab that spreads the whole building's load across its footprint. A common rule of thumb is that when footings would cover about half the building area, a mat becomes economical. It also resists differential settlement because the slab moves together, and it can sometimes replace both footings and floor slab, though it uses more concrete and steel.

    11. Which description fits a pile?¶

    1. A hole drilled, fitted with a reinforcing cage, and filled with concrete, sometimes with a flared bell
    2. A thick continuous slab that spreads a building's load over its whole footprint on weak soil
    3. A long, slender column installed by driving or screwing it into the ground, such as a steel H-pile
    4. A wide concrete pad under a single column, reaching below the frost depth to bear on firm soil
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Piles may be steel H-piles or pipe piles, precast concrete piles, or timber piles, and they are usually used in groups under a pile cap. Driving makes noise and vibration, so a quieter type may be chosen in a dense city. The drilled hole with a cage describes a drilled pier, the thick slab is a mat, and the pad under a column is a spread footing.

    See: Deep Foundations

    12. A contractor must support heavy columns near occupied buildings where neighbors object to noise and vibration. Which choice and associated concern fit best?¶

    1. Driven piles, which are quieter than drilling, but they are limited to very light columns and short spans
    2. Drilled piers, which need no inspection because the concrete is placed under pressure and cannot be contaminated
    3. Driven piles, which have a flared bell that increases end bearing but must be inspected open before placing concrete
    4. Drilled piers, which can carry a heavy column quietly, but caving soil and water in the hole must be controlled
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A drilled pier is formed by drilling, placing a steel cage, and filling with concrete, and a bell at the base increases end-bearing area. Drilling is less noisy than driving, and one pier can carry a heavy column. The shafts must be inspected before concrete is placed because groundwater and caving soil can contaminate it. Driven piles are verified by blows per foot.

    13. Why is a vented crawl space often replaced by a sealed, conditioned one in cold climates?¶

    1. Vents allow groundwater to enter the space, which raises hydrostatic pressure on the floor
    2. Vents admit cold air that chills the floor and humid summer air that condenses on cool surfaces
    3. Vents reduce the height of the space below the 18 in. minimum height required by the building code
    4. Vents interfere with the insulation of the floor, which must be left uncovered so that air can pass
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A crawl space is a shallow unfinished space, usually 18 to 48 in high, beneath the first floor. Traditional vents let in cold air and moist summer air, causing the moisture and air-movement problems explained in Chapter 4. Modern practice is often to seal the space, cover the ground with heavy polyethylene, insulate the walls, and condition the space.

    14. A retaining wall is rebuilt at twice its original height with the same backfill. By what factor does the lateral force per foot of wall increase?¶

    1. 2
    2. 8
    3. 4
    4. 16
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The force on a retaining wall is F = ½ γ h², which grows with the square of the height, so doubling the height quadruples the force. A factor of 2 would apply to a linear relationship, 8 to a cube, and 16 to a fourth power. A retaining wall holds back soil at a change in ground level and has no floor to brace it at the top.

    15. Suppose the drainage fails and water fills the backfill behind an 8 ft basement wall designed for dry soil. What is the most important consequence?¶

    1. Water alone adds about 499 psf at the base, more than the drained soil, so the wall can crack
    2. The water lowers the lateral pressure because it carries part of the soil's weight on the wall
    3. The water has no effect on the wall, since concrete is unaffected by moisture or by pressure
    4. The water adds uplift on the roof framing, which must resist it with metal straps and hold-downs
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A perimeter drain, a perforated pipe in washed gravel wrapped in fabric, removes water before it can build pressure. Without it, water at 62.4 pcf gives about 499 psf at the base and about 1,997 lb per foot, which is already more than the 1,440 lb per foot from drained soil, and the soil still adds its own pressure. Drainage lowers the pressure and waterproofing resists what remains.
